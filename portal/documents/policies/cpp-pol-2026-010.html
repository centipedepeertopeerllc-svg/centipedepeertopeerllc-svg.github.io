Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 1. Why This Exists 1.1 Centipede’s shelter and homeless outreach programs serve people who have children — including families living outside. Wherever children are, their safety is our business. 1.2 California law requires suspected child abuse and neglect to be reported to the proper authorities. This procedure makes sure every Centipede staff member, volunteer, and peer supporter knows exactly how to do that — quickly, correctly, and without fear. 1.3 This procedure works alongside the Shelter Security Policy (CSP-SEC-001). Where they overlap on child safety, this procedure governs the reporting steps. 2. What Must Be Reported 2.1 The standard is reasonable suspicion — not certainty. You do not need proof. You do not need to be sure. If what you see or hear gives you reasonable suspicion that a child (anyone under 18) is being abused or neglected, you report it. Let the professionals investigate — that is their job, not yours. 2.2 In plain language, reportable abuse and neglect includes: • Physical abuse — a child with injuries that do not match the explanation: bruises, burns, broken bones, or a pattern of injuries. • Sexual abuse — any sexual contact with a child, or sexual exploitation of a child, by anyone. • Neglect — failure to provide adequate food, shelter, supervision, or medical care, putting the child at risk. Being homeless is not itself neglect — many homeless parents care for their children lovingly. Report the conditions you observe, not the family’s housing status. • Willful cruelty or unjustifiable punishment — deliberately inflicting pain or suffering on a child. 2.3 When in doubt, report. It is never wrong to make a good-faith report. It can be very wrong not to. Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 3. How to Report 3.1 If a child is in immediate danger, call 911 first. Then make the CPS report. 3.2 Call the San Joaquin County CPS 24-hour hotline: (209) 468-1333 — immediately, the same day you learn of the concern. Do not wait for a supervisor’s permission. Do not wait until morning. 3.3 The 36-hour rule. California law requires a written follow-up report within 36 hours of the phone report. Complete the written report the same day whenever possible — never later than 36 hours. Remember: phone call immediately — written report within 36 hours. Both are required. 4. What Information to Have Ready 4.1 Gather what you can without delaying the call. Never hold the report to collect more information. Have ready: • The child’s name, age, and where the child is right now. • The parent or guardian’s name and contact information, if known. • What you observed — facts only: what you saw, what you heard, when, and where. No opinions, no diagnoses, no conclusions. • Your name and contact information as the reporter. 4.2 If you do not know something, say so. An incomplete report made now is worth more than a complete report made too late. Tell the CEO the same day. The CEO is notified of every CPS report — no exceptions. 2 Do NOT investigate yourself. Do not question the child further, do not gather evidence, do not 3 try to prove or disprove anything. That is CPS’s job. Do NOT confront the family. Confrontation can put the child in greater danger and compromise 4 the investigation. Do NOT promise a child confidentiality about the report. Never tell a child “I won’t tell 5 anyone” about abuse or neglect. You can say: “I care about you, and I have to make sure you’re safe — that means I have to tell people whose job is to protect kids.” 6. Your Protections 6.1 Confidentiality. A reporter’s identity is kept confidential by law. 6.2 Immunity. Anyone who makes a report in good faith is immune from civil and criminal liability — even if the investigation finds no abuse. 6.3 No retaliation, ever. No Centipede staff member or volunteer will ever face discipline, demotion, or removal for making a good-faith child safety report. Reporting a concern is always the right call. 7. A Note on Mandated Reporters 7.1 California Penal Code §11165.7 lists the professionals the state calls “mandated reporters.” Peer support specialists are not specifically named in that list. 7.2 However, shelter staff whose duties include direct contact with and supervision of children likely qualify as mandated reporters under the law — and mandated reporters who fail to report face criminal penalties. 7.3 The practical rule for every Centipede team member: when in doubt, report. Anyone — mandated reporter or not — may report suspected child abuse or neglect, and everyone on this team is expected to.
