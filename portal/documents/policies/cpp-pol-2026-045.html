CENTIPEDE PEER TO PEER, LLC CPP-POL-2026-045 — Outreach Worker Skills, Training, and Field Gear Policy Issued November 18, 2026, by direction of the Chief Executive Officer. 1. PURPOSE. This policy sets the standard for the skills every Centipede outreach worker is expected to bring to the field, the training and certifications the company works toward for its field staff, and the gear every outreach worker carries. Outreach is the front door of this company: the worker standing in front of a person in crisis is Centipede, in that moment. This policy states what that worker knows, how that worker is trained, and what that worker carries — so the standard is written, taught the same way to everyone, and held the same way for everyone. 2. SCOPE. This policy applies to every employee and volunteer who does outreach or field work for Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC, in every program — including Homeless Outreach, 1-on-1 Peer Support, Referrals & ID Assistance, Community Resource Navigation, and Hygiene Outreach — and to the supervisors who train and oversee them. It works alongside the Centipede Peer Support Manual (CPP-MAN-2026-001), which states how peer support is done at this company. 3. SKILL STANDARD ONE — TRAUMA-INFORMED COMMUNICATION AND EMPATHY. Every outreach worker communicates in a trauma-informed way, as SAMHSA's guidance on trauma-informed care directs and as Chapter 5 of the Peer Support Manual states it for this company. The worker builds trust with people in crisis: listens first and lets the person finish; speaks plainly and calmly; explains who he is, what Centipede is, and what he can and cannot do before anything else; offers choices instead of directions; and never pressures, corners, or rushes a person. Empathy in this company is a working skill, not a feeling: the worker meets the person where the person is, treats the person's own account of their situation as the starting point, and keeps healthy professional boundaries while doing it — warm and steady, never personally entangled, never promising what the company has not approved. Trauma treatment is clinical work and is outside Centipede's scope at all times; the outreach worker's part is a trauma-informed way of working and a warm referral when a need is beyond peer support. 4. SKILL STANDARD TWO — CRISIS DE-ESCALATION AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION. Every outreach worker is expected to stay calm under pressure and to handle challenging situations safely. The order of the work is: safety first, then calm, then conversation. The worker slows the situation down, lowers his voice instead of raising it, gives physical space, removes an audience where he can, and offers the person a next step small enough to take. The worker does not argue, threaten, physically intervene, or put hands on any person. If a situation cannot be made safe by words, the worker withdraws and calls for help: 911 for emergencies, 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) for a person in emotional crisis who will accept the call, and the Chief Executive Officer the same day in every case. Any incident — a threat, an injury, a call to 911, a situation the worker had to leave — is reported to the CEO the same day and documented under Section 7. 5. SKILL STANDARD THREE — RESOURCE NAVIGATION AND DOCUMENTATION. Every outreach worker knows the local ground he works: the housing programs and shelter routes in San Joaquin County, the food and healthcare partners the company works with, and the benefit systems the people we serve move through (including CalFresh, CalWORKs, SSI, and county Human Services Agency programs), and keeps that knowledge current as programs open, close, and change. Knowing a resource means knowing its real door — its address, its hours, who it serves, and what a person must bring — not just its name. Navigation is done with the person, at the person's direction: the worker connects, walks with, and follows up; the worker does not decide for the person. Every contact is documented the same day under the company's DAP standard (Data, Assessment, Plan — Peer Support Manual, Chapter 7): the person's own words as Data, the peer's observation as Assessment, and the next steps with an owner and a date as the Plan. The binders are the official record (CPP-POL-2026-044); a contact that is not documented the same day is work the record cannot show. 6. SKILL STANDARD FOUR — ADAPTABILITY, RESILIENCE, AND SELF-CARE. Outreach work shifts without notice: priorities change mid-shift, a planned visit becomes a crisis, and the physical demands of field work in all weather are real. Every outreach worker is expected to manage shifting priorities in the order the CEO's standing directions set — safety, then the person in front of him, then the schedule — and to raise a hand early when a shift's load exceeds what one worker can safely carry, rather than carrying it silently. Self-care is a company expectation, not a private extra: the worker eats, rests on his breaks, says plainly when the work has hit him, and uses supervision to process hard contacts. A worker who is running on empty is a safety risk to himself and to the people he serves; the company would rather hear "I need a day" than lose the worker. Burnout concerns go to the CEO directly and are received as workforce information, never as a failure. 7. TRAINING AND CERTIFICATION STANDARDS. The company trains every outreach worker in the four skill standards of this policy at onboarding and refreshes that training in supervision. The company's target certifications for field staff are: (a) First Aid / CPR, held current; and (b) Mental Health First Aid. The company's target trainings are: trauma-informed care, and crisis prevention and de-escalation training. Where a certification or training carries a cost, it is obtained through the purchase-order process (CPP-POL-2026-028 and CPP-POL-2026-029); no worker pays out of pocket for a company-required certification without the CEO's direction. One line is held plainly: Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC does not itself certify anyone. Peer support certification in California runs through the state route (CalMHSA), and company training under this policy is company training — it prepares a worker for the company's work and supports the state route, and it is never represented, to staff or to the public, as state certification. 8. FIELD GEAR — THE STANDARD KIT. Every outreach worker carries the standard field kit on outreach shifts: sturdy protective gloves (mechanic's or gardening gloves) for handling debris, encampment materials, and anything sharp or unsanitary; hygiene and safety supplies appropriate to the shift (such as hand sanitizer, wipes, and basic first-aid supplies); water; a charged phone; and the company's current resource information for the people he meets. Gloves are worn whenever the worker's hands go anywhere he cannot see or anything he did not place there — no exceptions for speed. Kit is company property, is checked and restocked through supervision, and shortages are reported rather than worked around. Damaged or contaminated gear is replaced, not reused. 9. PERSONAL SAFETY ITEMS — PEPPER SPRAY. [This section is marked for counsel review.] An employee may carry a personal pepper-spray device for self-defense only, carried and used strictly within California law (Penal Code section 22810 — container no larger than 2.5 ounces; defensive use only). Pepper spray is a personal defensive item, never a company weapon: the company does not issue it as a tool of the work, it is never used offensively, never used to compel, move, or punish any person, and its presence never substitutes for the withdrawal-and-call standard of Section 4. Any use of pepper spray by an employee on shift — any discharge, for any reason — is reported to the Chief Executive Officer the same day and documented in writing the same day. 10. SUPERVISION AND ESCALATION. Field staff work under the supervision structure of the Peer Support Manual (Chapter 12). Anything beyond a worker's training, anything touching safety, and anything a worker is unsure of goes up the same day — to the supervisor, and to the Chief Executive Officer wherever this policy or the Manual names him. Escalating is always the right call; no worker is ever faulted for bringing a matter up, and a worker who carries a matter alone that should have gone up will be retrained on this section. 11. MONEY AND SCOPE. This policy authorizes no purchase. Gear, training, and certification purchases follow the purchase-order process (CPP-POL-2026-028 and CPP-POL-2026-029). Nothing in this policy makes any Centipede service clinical: Centipede provides non-clinical peer support only, and no statement under this policy may promise an outcome to any person. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a California for-profit limited liability company; nothing in this policy states or implies nonprofit or charitable status.
