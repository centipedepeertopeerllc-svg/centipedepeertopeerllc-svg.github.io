Policy Review & Adoption — 7 1. Purpose & Scope 1.1 Purpose. This policy exists for one reason: the safety of every person connected to the Centipede shelter — members, staff, volunteers, visitors, and our neighbors in the surrounding community. 1.2 Scope. This policy applies to everyone on shelter property at all times, including the building, grounds, parking areas, and any off-site location where shelter staff are operating. It covers both the emergency shelter and the transitional housing programs. 1.3 Relationship to other documents. This policy works alongside the Letter of Expectations (the conduct commitments every member reads before entering), the Member Policy Handbook, the Housing Stability Services Manual, and the Mental Health Crisis Response Guide. Where they overlap, this policy governs safety decisions. 2. Guiding Principles • Dignity first. Every safety measure — a bag check, a curfew reminder, a removal — is carried out with respect for the person involved. We protect people; we do not punish them. • De-escalation before force. Words before hands, always. Physical intervention is a last resort, used only to prevent immediate physical harm. • Peer-led safety. Our safety team leads with lived experience. People who have been through crisis themselves are often the best at calming one. • Fairness. The rules apply equally to everyone in the house — member, staff, or volunteer. No favorites, no exceptions for position. The CEO holds ultimate authority for shelter safety, approves this policy, and reviews all serious incidents. 3.2 Shift Lead. Each shift has one designated Shift Lead who owns safety day-to-day: making on-the-spot safety decisions, directing de-escalation, deciding when to call 911 or the Stockton Police Department, and completing incident reports. 3.3 Peer Support Staff. De-escalate conflicts, support members in distress, keep the Shift Lead informed, and never leave a person in crisis alone. 3.4 Volunteers. Volunteers observe and report — they alert the Shift Lead to any safety concern immediately. Volunteers shall not physically intervene in a conflict except to prevent immediate physical harm to a person, and shall never conduct searches alone. 3.5 Members. Follow the Letter of Expectations, respect curfew and house rules, and report safety concerns to staff promptly. Members are our partners in keeping the house safe — not its subjects. 4. Entry, Exit & Curfew 4.1 Sign-in/sign-out. Every person entering or leaving the shelter signs in and out at the front desk, every time. No exceptions. 4.2 Curfew. Curfew is 9:00 PM. (This hour is adjustable by the CEO as operations require.) Members with verified work schedules may receive a written curfew exception from the Shift Lead. A member arriving after curfew without an approved exception may be denied re-entry for that night; staff shall offer an alternative referral (see Section 9.4) rather than leaving the person with nowhere to go. 4.3 Guests. Guests enter by staff permission only, sign in at the front desk, remain in common areas, and leave by 8:00 PM. No overnight guests. 4.4 Secured areas. Staff offices, medication storage, and utility rooms are locked at all times. Members do not enter secured areas unaccompanied. Prohibited Items & Conduct 5.1 Zero tolerance Consistent with the Letter of Expectations every member reads before entering, the following result in immediate removal and, where appropriate, notification of law enforcement: • Any act of violence or credible threat of violence toward any person. • Possession of a weapon of any kind on shelter property. • Theft from members, staff, volunteers, or the shelter. 5.2 Clean and sober property No drugs or alcohol on shelter property — no exceptions. A member found using on the property must leave the property, consistent with the Letter of Expectations. Struggling with addiction never disqualifies a person from our care; staff shall offer a treatment referral on the way out. Prescription medications must be declared at intake and stored securely by staff. 5.3 Searches & bag checks To keep the house safe, staff may conduct bag checks at entry and when there is reasonable concern about prohibited items. Searches are conducted respectfully and with dignity safeguards: • Personal searches are conducted by a staff member of the same gender as the person being checked, in a private area, with a second staff member present as witness. • The person is told plainly what is happening and why, before it happens. • A person who refuses a search is denied entry — that is the only consequence of refusal. No one is searched by force. • Volunteers never conduct searches alone; a trained staff member must be present. Prohibited items found during a check are secured by staff and returned at departure, except weapons and illegal substances, which are turned over to law enforcement. Speak low and slow. Keep your hands visible and your body relaxed. • Listen first. Let the person say what is wrong before trying to fix anything. • Offer choices, not orders: “Would you like to step outside with me for some air?” • Never raise your voice, never crowd the person, never touch them without permission. Step 2 — Separate and cool down If words are not working, separate the people involved. Move them to different spaces. Give everyone time and quiet. Most conflicts end here. Step 3 — Shift Lead decides The Shift Lead assesses: Is anyone unsafe right now? Can this be resolved in-house? The Shift Lead may issue a warning, arrange a mediated conversation, or move to removal under Section 9. Step 4 — Call for help when needed • Call 911 immediately for: weapons, active violence, serious injury, or any imminent threat to life. • Stockton Police Department non-emergency line for non-urgent criminal matters (theft discovered after the fact, trespassers who have left). Centipede maintains a cooperative working relationship with the Stockton Police Department; officers are called as partners in safety, not as punishment. Step 5 — After the incident Check on everyone involved, including witnesses. Complete the incident report the same day (Section 8). Follow up with affected members within 24 hours — a crisis does not end when the shouting stops. Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 7. Medical & Mental-Health Crises Centipede is a non-clinical, peer-based program. Staff do not diagnose, treat, or restrain — they recognize, respond, and refer. • Medical emergency (overdose, chest pain, seizure, serious injury): call 911 immediately. If naloxone is available and an opioid overdose is suspected, trained staff may administer it. • Suicide crisis: call or text 988. Take every statement seriously. Stay with the person — do not leave them alone — until professional help arrives. • Physical restraint is never used except to prevent immediate physical harm to a person, and then only the minimum necessary until help arrives. • Mandatory reporting: suspected abuse or neglect of a child, elder, or dependent adult is reported as required by California law. Full crisis procedures are detailed in the Mental Health Crisis Response Guide, which all safety staff must read before their first shift. 8. Incident Reporting & Documentation • Every incident — conflict, injury, search, removal, medical or mental-health crisis, law-enforcement contact — is written up the same day by the Shift Lead. • The CEO reviews every serious incident report within 24 hours. • Reports are factual: what happened, who was involved, what was done. No opinions, no diagnoses, no clinical language. • Reports are filed securely with access limited to authorized staff. Member information in reports is confidential, disclosed only as required by law, to prevent imminent serious harm, or to report suspected abuse or neglect. • The CEO is notified immediately — by phone, any hour — of: any 911 call, any weapon, any injury requiring medical care, any removal, and any media presence. Consequences Consequences are progressive, tied to the Letter of Expectations, and always delivered with dignity: • First concern: a private conversation and a written reminder of the expectation. • Continued violations: temporary removal for a defined period, with a written notice stating the reason, the length, and the right to appeal under the grievance procedure. • Immediate removal: violence, credible threats, weapons, or theft — the zero-tolerance items from the Letter of Expectations — result in immediate removal and, where appropriate, law-enforcement notification. There is no warning step for these. 9.4 No one into the night. No member is removed into the night without staff offering an alternative referral where possible — a partner shelter, a crisis line (211), or transportation to a safe location. Safety does not end at our door. 9.5 Return. Removed members may request re-entry review by the CEO after the removal period. Removal for violence is reviewed case by case; the safety of the house comes first. 10. Policy Review & Adoption • This policy is reviewed by the Board of Directors at least annually, and after any serious incident that reveals a gap. • The CEO may issue interim safety updates between reviews; all interim updates are reported to the Board at its next meeting. • This policy takes effect upon the opening of the Centipede shelter and is subject to formal adoption by the Board of Directors.
