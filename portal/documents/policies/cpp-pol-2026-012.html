Purpose Facing court alone — especially while homeless, in recovery, or in mental-health distress — keeps people from showing up and from being heard. This policy governs the Centipede Justice Support Program: peer-based court support in which a trained peer supporter attends court with a member as a supportive presence in the public gallery. The program’s goals are to reduce fear and confusion, support court appearance, and connect members with recovery and community resources at every step. 2. Scope This policy applies to all Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC staff, peer supporters, volunteers, and contractors who participate in any Justice Support Program activity, including court accompaniment, court-date reminders, factual participation letters, coordination with outside parties, and post-court follow-up. 3. Definitions Member — a person enrolled in Centipede peer support services who requests Justice Support Program assistance. Peer supporter — a trained Centipede staff member or volunteer, with lived experience of recovery, mental- health challenges, or homelessness, assigned to accompany and support a member. Court accompaniment — a peer supporter’s in-person attendance at a member’s court proceeding as a supportive presence, with the member’s signed consent. Factual letter — a written statement confirming only what peer staff have directly observed about a member’s participation in peer support and recovery activities. Factual letters contain no opinions about guilt, innocence, or sentencing. Eligibility • Members with pending criminal, traffic, or civil court matters in San Joaquin County who request peer support. • Participation is voluntary and begins only with the member’s signed Court Support Consent and Authorization. • A member may withdraw from the program at any time without penalty or loss of other Centipede services. 5. Roles and Responsibilities Chief Executive Officer — overall accountability for the program; assigns peer supporters (or designates an assigner); approves factual letters before issuance; ensures legal review and board adoption. Program lead / supervisor — day-to-day oversight: confirming court dates, times, departments, and locations; reviewing documentation; ensuring boundaries are observed; serving as the escalation point for concerns. Peer supporter — meets with the member before court; attends proceedings as a supportive presence; provides general, non-legal navigation information (practical logistics only — where to go and park, where to sit, when to stand, how to address the judge; never explanations of charges, pleas, or what might happen in the case); gives court-date reminders; records factual support notes; conducts post-court follow-up. Peer supporters do not give legal advice and do not speak for the member on the record unless invited by the court. 6. Procedures 6.1 — Intake and consent • Staff receive the member’s request and complete a Court Support Consent and Authorization form: permission to attend proceedings, and the specific information-sharing the member authorizes. • Staff provide the member a copy of the Member Handbook and confirm understanding of program boundaries. • No accompaniment or information-sharing occurs before signed consent is on file. 6.2 — Court accompaniment • The CEO or designee assigns the peer supporter and confirms date, time, department, and location. • Supporter and member meet beforehand to review logistics, discuss what to expect, and answer non-legal questions. • The supporter attends with the member, dressed and conducted appropriately for court, as a calm, non- judgmental presence before, during, and after the appearance. Court accompaniment is subject at all times to applicable court rules, security requirements, courtroom procedures, and the direction or order of the Court. A signed Centipede consent form does not by itself create a right to enter or participate in any courtroom proceeding. They describe only what staff directly observed. 6.4 — Factual participation and progress letters • Letters are issued only with the member’s signed authorization and only after CEO approval. • Letters state facts (dates of participation, activities attended, support provided). They never address guilt, innocence, sentencing, or legal strategy. Letters are addressed to the member’s defense counsel only, never directly to the court or a judge; counsel decides whether and how to use them. • A copy of every letter is retained in the member’s program file. 6.5 — Coordination with outside parties • With the member’s consent, staff may communicate with the member’s defense counsel, probation officer, and treatment providers — limited to scheduling, attendance, and support-service logistics, never the substance of the case, the charges, the evidence, or legal strategy. • Staff do not initiate ex parte contact with judges, prosecutors, or court staff about a member’s case. • All coordination is limited to the scope of the member’s signed authorization. 6.6 — Post-court follow-up • The supporter debriefs with the member after the appearance, connects the member with next-step resources, and documents the follow-up. • If a subsequent appearance is scheduled, the reminder and accompaniment cycle restarts at 6.2. 7. Prohibited Conduct and Boundaries The following are prohibited for all program staff, without exception: • Providing legal advice or legal representation, interpreting the law, predicting outcomes, or advising members on pleas, testimony, or legal strategy. All legal questions are referred to the member’s attorney or the Public Defender. • Speaking on the record on a member’s behalf unless expressly invited to do so by the court. • Ex parte communication with judges, prosecutors, or court staff about a member’s case. • Guaranteeing or promising case outcomes, sentencing, or release decisions. • Interfering with court proceedings, court orders, or the work of counsel. Confidentiality, Information Release, and Records Retention • All program records are confidential peer-support records. • No information about a member’s case is shared with the court, counsel, or any outside party without the member’s signed authorization, except as required by law, including subpoenas, court orders, and other legally required disclosures. • Limits of confidentiality — including imminent risk of harm and suspected abuse or neglect of a child, elder, or dependent adult — are explained to the member at intake and stated in the Member Handbook. • Program files are stored securely with access limited to authorized staff. Records are retained per the company’s records-retention schedule and applicable law. 9. Training Requirements • Peer supporters complete program orientation before their first assignment: this policy, the Member Handbook, courtroom conduct, documentation standards, boundaries, and crisis escalation (peer → supervisor → 988; 911 for immediate danger). • Refresher training is provided at least annually and whenever this policy is revised. 10. Compliance and Reporting Concerns • Any staff member who observes a boundary violation, unauthorized disclosure, or other misconduct reports it promptly to the program lead and the CEO. • Members may raise concerns directly with any staff member or the CEO without fear of losing services. Concerns are documented and reviewed. • Violations of this policy may result in reassignment, retraining, or removal from program duties. 11. Program Review This policy is reviewed at least annually and after any significant incident, and revised as needed with legal counsel input.
