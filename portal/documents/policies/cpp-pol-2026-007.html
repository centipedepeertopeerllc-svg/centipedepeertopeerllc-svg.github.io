Purpose and Scope This policy applies to all team members — employees, trainees, contractors, and volunteers. It ensures everyone receives the meal and rest breaks required by California law, while protecting the integrity of our client work. When we are with clients or out in the field, our full attention belongs to the people we serve. Breaks happen at designated locations — never during active outreach. 2. California Meal Break Law Cal. Labor Code § 512(a): An employer must provide a 30-minute unpaid meal period to any employee who works more than 5 hours per day. The meal period must begin before the end of the 5th hour of work. A second 30-minute meal period is required for shifts over 10 hours, beginning before the end of the 10th hour. • Meal breaks are unpaid and duty-free — the team member must be fully relieved of all duties. • Centipede does not use on-duty meal periods. Every meal break is a full, uninterrupted 30 minutes away from work. • If a meal break is missed, shortened, or interrupted, the team member is owed one hour of pay at their regular rate as a premium penalty. Cal. Labor Code § 226.7: Failure to provide a compliant meal or rest break triggers a premium payment of one additional hour of pay at the employee's regular rate — per violation, per day. These penalties add up fast. 3. California Rest Break Law IWC Wage Order 4-2001, § 12: Employers must authorize and permit a paid 10-minute rest period for every 4 hours worked, or major fraction thereof. Rest breaks should be taken in the middle of each work period when practical. • Rest breaks are paid time — team members stay on the clock. • A standard 8-hour shift includes two paid 10-minute rest breaks. • Rest breaks cannot be combined with meal breaks or saved until end of shift to leave early. Brinker Restaurant Corp. v. Superior Court (2012) 53 Cal.4th 1004: The employer's duty is to provide breaks — to relieve the employee of duty and not interfere — not to guarantee every break is taken. But the opportunity must be real, and company policy cannot discourage or prevent breaks. 4. Field Rule — No Breaks During Client Contact Team members may not take meal or rest breaks while actively working with clients or while deployed in the field away from a designated break location. This protects both the client relationship and the team member. Outreach requires full presence — stepping away mid-contact breaks trust and can leave a vulnerable person unsupported. All breaks are taken at designated break locations only. Field supervisors will plan outreach routes so team members return to a break location at the scheduled times. Break Check-In — Field Safety Because our teams work in the field with vulnerable populations, every team member must text or call the field supervisor when beginning and ending each meal and rest break. This is a safety measure, not surveillance — we need to know where everyone is, especially during downtime when they are not with a partner. • Check in: "On break — [location] — [time]" • Check out: "Back on — [time]" • If a team member does not check out within 10 minutes of their break ending, the supervisor follows up immediately. 8. Heat Illness Prevention — Additional Breaks Cal. Code Regs., tit. 8, § 3395: California's Heat Illness Prevention standard requires employers of outdoor workers to provide cool-down rest periods, shade, and water. When temperatures reach 95°F, additional protective measures apply. On any day the temperature reaches 95°F or above, each team member receives one additional paid 10-minute cool-down break in the afternoon, taken at a shaded break location with water available. Team members are encouraged to take preventative cool-down rests as needed — no one will be penalized for protecting their health. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC | 717 E. The log is submitted to the field supervisor at end of shift and filed with timekeeping records. This protects both the team member and the company in the event of a wage claim. 10. Missed or Interrupted Breaks If an emergency prevents a scheduled break, the supervisor will provide a make-up break during the same shift. If a compliant break cannot be provided, the team member receives the § 226.7 premium — one hour of pay at their regular rate — and the incident is documented with the reason. 11. Supervisor Responsibilities • Plan daily routes so every team member can reach a break location on schedule. • Assign staggered break groups each morning. • Never assign or expect work during a meal or rest break. • Monitor break check-ins and follow up on any missed check-out. • Ensure unpaid meal breaks are accurately recorded in timekeeping, consistent with the Employee Privacy Policy. • On days reaching 95°F, activate the additional cool-down break. 12. No Retaliation No team member will face retaliation, discipline, or adverse action for taking a meal or rest break under this policy, for reporting a missed break, or for taking a preventative cool-down rest on a hot day.
