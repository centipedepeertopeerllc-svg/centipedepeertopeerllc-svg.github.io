CENTIPEDE PEER TO PEER, LLC CPP-POL-2026-029 — EMPLOYEE PURCHASE ORDER PROCEDURE Adopted at the direction of Levi Allen Spears, Chief Executive Officer, November 17, 2026. Board notification: November 27, 2026 board meeting. Companion to CPP-POL-2026-028 (Purchase Order Approval and Timing Policy). Prepared by Jarvis, Board Officer / Record Keeper. 1. WHO THIS COVERS. Every employee who needs the company to buy anything — for the company, for a program, or for a client — uses a Purchase Order (Form CPP-PO). If you are spending company or program money, there is a PO first. NO PO, NO PURCHASE. 2. HOW TO GET A PO FORM. Blank Form CPP-PO is available from the company office (ask Jarvis, Record Keeper). CLIENT POs can also be started and filled out in the client dashboard, under the client's record — a completed dashboard PO prints as the official form and joins the binder filing queue. Use one PO per purchase, and take the next PO number in sequence — never reuse a number. 3. HOW TO FILL OUT THE FORM. Section 1 — The purchase: vendor or seller and how to reach them; exactly what is being bought or what service is being done; quantity and price (attach the quote, listing, or invoice the price came from); when it is needed; what program, card, or account pays; and the purpose in plain words. Section 2 — Client information (CLIENT purchases ONLY): the client's name, client ID / binder number, program, what the client needs, why it is needed, and whether the client asked for or agreed to the purchase, with the date. Leave Section 2 BLANK on company and program POs — client information never goes on a company PO. Section 3 — Request and approval: your name and title as requester, and the date. Approval and any CEO countersignature happen here (see CPP-POL-2026-028 for who approves and how long it takes). Section 4 — Receipt: after the purchase, record the completion date, attach the invoice or receipt, and write the final amount paid. A PO is not finished until Section 4 is done. 4. COMPANY POs AND CLIENT POs — THE SEPARATION. (a) A COMPANY or PROGRAM PO is about the company's business. It names no client and carries no client information, ever. (b) A CLIENT PO is about one named client's need. It always completes Section 2, it is confidential, and it is filed in the Client POs binder, in that client's binder, and in the client dashboard. (c) If a purchase touches both — company supplies bought on the same trip as a client's item — that is TWO POs, not one. 5. ROUTING AND TIMING. Send the completed PO to the acting PO approver (Eric Jacobson, until a Financial Officer is appointed). Standard approval: up to two weeks. Emergency PO — health or safety, an essential home service out, an immovable deadline — mark it EMERGENCY, state the ground, and approval comes within up to two business days (CPP-POL-2026-028 §5). Do not place the order or book the service until the PO comes back approved, except where the CEO directs otherwise in writing for a stated emergency. 6. HARD LINES. - Never write a Social Security number or a full ID number on a PO. - Never split one purchase across POs to stay under the $250 CEO countersignature limit. - Receipts are not optional: no receipt, the PO stays open and follows you. - Client information stays inside the company: outside disclosure only on the client's signed Release of Information or a judge-signed court order.
