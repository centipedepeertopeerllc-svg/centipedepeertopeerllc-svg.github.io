CENTIPEDE PEER TO PEER, LLC CPP-POL-2026-048 — Mandated Reporter Policy Issued November 18, 2026, by direction of the Chief Executive Officer. 1. PURPOSE. Centipede's staff work alongside people the law singles out for protection — children, elders, and dependent adults — in their homes, in shelters, and on the street. California law requires reports of known or reasonably suspected abuse and neglect of those people, made fast and made to the agencies charged with acting on them. This policy states the company's rule, which is simple and stricter than the legal floor: every Centipede staff member reports as a mandated reporter. It sets the steps, the clocks, the training, and the records, so that no staff member ever has to wonder what to do in the moment it matters. 2. SCOPE. This policy applies to every employee of Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC, in every program, and — as a matter of company policy — to volunteers working in company programs. It covers suspected abuse and neglect of children under 18, of elders 65 and older, and of dependent adults 18 to 64, encountered in any company work: peer support, outreach, resource navigation, shelter and hygiene work, youth and family programming, and every other program the company runs. It works alongside the HIPAA and California Confidentiality standard, the program policies, and the Centipede Peer Support Manual (CPP-MAN-2026-001), and it is carried in plain language in the staff guide, "California Mandated Reporter — What It Is, How It Works, What You Do," filed in the Mandated Reporter binder. 3. LEGAL BASIS. [This policy is marked for counsel review.] The Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting Act (CANRA), Penal Code sections 11164 through 11174.3, requires mandated reporters — the categories listed in Penal Code section 11165.7 — to report known or reasonably suspected child abuse or neglect. The Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act, Welfare and Institutions Code sections 15600 through 15675, requires mandated reporters — defined in section 15630(a) to include any person who has assumed full or intermittent responsibility for the care or custody of an elder or dependent adult, and care custodians under section 15610.17 — to report known or reasonably suspected abuse of an elder or dependent adult. Business and Professions Code section 18975 (AB 506) requires training in child abuse and neglect identification and reporting for administrators, employees, and regular volunteers of youth service organizations. The company states this basis plainly, including where the statutes' coverage of a given job title is a matter of duties rather than labels; Section 4 removes the question from company practice by adopting the duty for everyone. 4. THE COMPANY RULE — EVERY STAFF MEMBER REPORTS. Every Centipede staff member is a mandated reporter by company policy, regardless of whether a statute names his or her exact job category. No staff member may reason that a report is someone else's job because of a title, a program, or a question about which statutory category fits. Where the law plainly covers a staff member — for example, staff whose duties require direct contact and supervision of children (Penal Code section 11165.7(a)(8)), employees of a licensed community care facility (section 11165.7(a)(10)), and any staff member who has assumed responsibility for the care or custody of an elder or dependent adult (Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(a)) — the duty is the law's own, and this policy runs with it. Where the law's coverage could be debated, the company's rule settles it: report. 5. WHAT MUST BE REPORTED. A report is required when a staff member, in the course of company work, knows of, observes, or reasonably suspects: for a child — physical injury by other than accidental means, sexual abuse or exploitation, willful harming or endangering, cruel or inhuman corporal punishment or injury, or neglect, including a caretaker's failure to provide adequate food, clothing, shelter, medical care, or supervision; for an elder or dependent adult — physical abuse including sexual abuse, neglect including self-neglect, abandonment, abduction, isolation, financial abuse, or other treatment resulting in physical harm, pain, or mental suffering, including a care custodian's deprivation of needed goods or services. Reasonable suspicion is the standard: facts that would lead a reasonable person in the staff member's position to suspect abuse or neglect. Proof is not required and investigation is not the staff member's task. Poverty or homelessness, by itself, is not neglect; the question is always harm, risk of harm, or unmet basic care. 6. HOW TO REPORT — A CHILD. The report is made in two steps, in this order. First, telephone immediately, or as soon as practicably possible: San Joaquin County Child Protective Services, (209) 468-1333 (24 hours), or a law enforcement agency (Penal Code section 11166(a)). Second, within 36 hours of the telephone report, send the written report on form BCIA 8572, Suspected Child Abuse Report, to the agency that received the call, completing it even where some information is unknown (Penal Code section 11167(a)). If a child is in immediate danger, call 911 before anything else. 7. HOW TO REPORT — AN ELDER OR DEPENDENT ADULT. The report is made in two steps, in this order. First, telephone immediately, or as soon as practicably possible: San Joaquin County Adult Protective Services, (209) 468-3780 or toll-free (888) 800-4800 (24 hours), or a law enforcement agency (Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(b)). Second, within two working days of the telephone report, send the written report on form SOC 341, Report of Suspected Dependent Adult/Elder Abuse, to the agency that received the call — for San Joaquin County APS, fax (209) 932-2641 or mail to San Joaquin County Human Services Agency, P.O. Box 201056, Stockton, CA 95201. Where the abuse is alleged to have occurred in a long-term care facility, the report goes to the Long-Term Care Ombudsman or to law enforcement. If the person is in immediate danger, call 911 before anything else. The clocks differ by law — 36 hours for a child report, two working days for an elder or dependent adult report — and staff are trained on both. 8. NO PERMISSION, NO DELAY, NO IMPEDING. A report under this policy is the individual staff member's own duty and requires no one's approval. No supervisor, administrator, or officer of the company — including the Chief Executive Officer — may impede or inhibit a report; impeding a report is a crime under both statutes (Penal Code section 11166(i); Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(h)). No company instruction, confidentiality practice, or client relationship delays or conditions a report. Where two or more staff members are present together and share the same knowledge, they may designate one member to make the report for the team as Penal Code section 11166(a) allows; the designation is recorded in the report log, and if there is any doubt that the report was made, each staff member reports. 9. NOTICE TO THE CEO — ALONGSIDE, NEVER INSTEAD. The staff member who makes a report notifies the Chief Executive Officer the same day, after the official report, stating the date, the agency, and the form filed. The notice exists so the company can support the staff member and keep its records straight. It is never a request for approval, it never precedes the official report, and the CEO's response — or the CEO being unreachable — never affects the report, which stands on its own. 10. CONFIDENTIALITY AND IMMUNITY. Reports and reporter identities are confidential under Penal Code section 11167.5 and Welfare and Institutions Code section 15633, and are shared inside the company only as Section 9 and Section 13 provide. A staff member who reports under this policy is immune from civil and criminal liability for the report as the statutes provide (Penal Code section 11172; Welfare and Institutions Code section 15634). The company does not retaliate against any staff member for making a report, ever, for any reason. 11. FAILURE TO REPORT. Under the statutes, a mandated reporter who fails to report commits a misdemeanor punishable by up to six months in county jail, a fine of up to $1,000, or both (Penal Code section 11166(c); Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(h)); a willful failure, where the abuse results in death or great bodily injury, carries up to one year in county jail and a fine of up to $5,000. Under this policy, failure to report is also a serious breach of company duty, addressed under the company's personnel rules up to and including termination. 12. TRAINING AND CERTIFICATION. Every staff member completes the California Mandated Reporter Training — the General Training course provided free by the California Department of Social Services, Office of Child Abuse Prevention, at mandatedreporterca.com (about four hours) — within 30 days of the staff member's start date. Staff already working when this policy issued complete it by December 18, 2026. The staff member saves and prints the state certificate of completion on finishing and delivers it to the Record Keeper, who files it in the staff member's HR record. On completion, the company also issues its Mandated Reporter certificate under the Chief Executive Officer as trainer, bearing the Mandated Reporter seal; a copy is filed in the HR record with the state certificate. Staff and regular volunteers whose duties place them with children in a youth-serving program also complete the training Business and Professions Code section 18975 requires, through the same state site, before working with children unsupervised. Training is taken on company time as scheduled by the supervisor; the duty to report, however, runs from the first day of work whether or not the training is yet complete. Refresher training is completed every two years, alongside the continuing education cycle the company already keeps. Nothing in this policy makes any Centipede service clinical, and the Medi-Cal Peer Support Specialist certification's law-and-ethics continuing education (20 hours every two years, including six hours of law and ethics, per DHCS and CalMHSA) runs alongside this policy without replacing it. 13. RECORDS. The Record Keeper keeps the Mandated Reporter Report Log: for each report, the date and time of the telephone report, the agency that received it, the written form filed and the date it was sent, and the staff member who reported. The log records the fact of a report, nothing more; it is not an investigation file and holds no findings. Copies of written reports are kept with the client's record under the company's confidentiality rules, and the log is filed in the Mandated Reporter binder with a copy in the Legal Binder. The content of a report is disclosed only as the reporting statutes allow. Each staff member signs, at hire and on this policy's issue for current staff, the statements acknowledging the requirement to report — form LIC 9108 for child abuse and form SOC 341A for elder and dependent adult abuse — filed in the staff member's HR record, in the pattern of Penal Code section 11166.5 and Welfare and Institutions Code section 15659. 14. COUNSEL REVIEW. Sections 3, 6, and 7 of this policy rest on the reporting statutes, and this policy is marked for counsel review at the company's next counsel touchpoint, the same treatment CPP-POL-2026-045 gives its Section 9 and CPP-POL-2026-047 gives its Sections 3 and 10. The rest of this policy states the company's own working standard — everyone reports, the call comes first, the written report follows on the statute's clock, and the record is kept — and stands on the company's own authority to set it. 15. MONEY AND SCOPE. This policy authorizes no purchase. The training it requires is the state's free training; the forms it uses are the state's free forms. Nothing in this policy states or implies nonprofit or charitable status: Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a California for-profit limited liability company. Board notification of this policy is set for the board meeting of November 27, 2026. 16. THE MANDATED REPORTER SEAL ON LETTERHEAD. (Added November 18, 2026, by direction of the Chief Executive Officer.) Every person certified under this policy's Mandated Reporter certification — that is, every holder of the company's Mandated Reporter certificate issued under Section 12 — must place the Mandated Reporter seal on anything he or she writes on company letterhead, alongside his or her own seal. The requirement is system-wide: it applies in every program and on every letterhead document a certified person authors, from the first document written after certification. The Mandated Reporter seal stands alongside the writer's own seal in the same manner as the company's other seals. It is used only by a person certified under this certification, and never before the certificate issues. A letterhead document written by a certified person without the Mandated Reporter seal is incomplete under this policy and is corrected and reissued before it goes out.
