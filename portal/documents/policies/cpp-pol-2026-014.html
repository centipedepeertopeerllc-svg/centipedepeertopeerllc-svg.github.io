Purpose, Scope & Definitions 1.1 Purpose This policy exists for one reason: the safety of every person connected to Centipede — the people we serve, our staff, our volunteers, our visitors, and our neighbors. Every person who walks with us — in a camp, on a street, or through our future shelter doors — deserves to go home safe. This policy is how we keep that promise: clear rules, fairly applied, with dignity in every interaction. 1.2 Scope • Field outreach (in effect on board adoption). All Centipede outreach operations — camp visits, street outreach, joint runs with law enforcement, and any off-site location where our people are working. • Shelter (in effect when the shelter opens). Everyone on shelter property at all times — building, grounds, and parking areas — covering emergency shelter and transitional housing. 1.3 Relationship to other documents This policy works alongside the Letter of Expectations, the Member Policy Handbook, the Housing Stability Services Manual, and the Mental Health Crisis Response Guide. Where they overlap on safety, this policy governs. 1.4 Definitions • Member — a person receiving Centipede services. • Staff — Centipede employees, including peer support specialists. • Volunteer — an unpaid person serving under staff supervision. • Shift Lead — the designated safety authority for a shelter shift (when the shelter opens). • Field Team Lead — the designated safety authority for an outreach outing. • Serious incident — any 911 call, weapon, injury requiring medical care, removal, or law-enforcement contact. Holds ultimate authority for safety, approves this policy, and reviews every serious incident report within 24 hours. • Board Officer (Executive Assistant to the CEO). Maintains this policy document, supports incident review, and assists the CEO on safety matters. • Shift Lead (shelter). Owns safety shift-to-shift: on-the-spot safety decisions, directing de-escalation, deciding when to call 911 or law enforcement, and completing incident reports. • Field Team Lead (outreach). Owns safety in the field: the day's route and area plan, check-ins, go / no- go calls on scenes, 911 and law-enforcement calls, and incident reports. • Peer Support Staff. De-escalate conflicts, support people in distress, keep the lead informed, and never leave a person in crisis alone. • Volunteers. Observe and report — alert the lead to any safety concern immediately. Volunteers do not physically intervene in a conflict except to prevent immediate physical harm to a person, and never conduct searches alone. • Members. Follow the Letter of Expectations, respect house rules, and report safety concerns to staff promptly. Members are our partners in keeping the house safe — not its subjects. 3. Guiding Principles • Dignity first. Every safety measure — a bag check, a curfew reminder, a removal — is carried out with respect for the person involved. We protect people; we do not punish them. • De-escalation before force. Words before hands, always. Physical intervention is a last resort, used only to prevent immediate physical harm. • We are peers, not police. Centipede staff are not law enforcement officers, not first responders, and not security guards. Staff have no police powers: no arrests, no pursuits, no detentions, no citizen's-arrest heroics. We do safety, not enforcement. • People first, property second. No belonging — ours or anyone's — is worth a life. Walk away from property disputes. • Fairness. The rules apply equally to everyone — member, staff, or volunteer. No favorites, no exceptions for position. Why this matters for our partners: because our people know exactly what they will and will not do, law enforcement and partner agencies can plan joint work with us confidently — clear roles, no surprises, no one freelancing. Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 4. Field Outreach Safety Our outreach teams walk into camps and onto streets where life is unpredictable. These rules are how they come home safe. 4.1 Buddy system • No one goes alone. No staff member enters a camp or conducts outreach solo — minimum two people on every outing. • Volunteers are always paired with trained staff, never sent out on their own. 4.2 Check-in protocol • Before every outing, the Field Team Lead logs the planned areas and route, the team members, and the expected return time. • Teams check in with the lead by phone or text at least once every hour while in the field. • Missed check-in: the lead calls the team immediately. If there is no answer within 15 minutes, the lead notifies the CEO and, if the team cannot be reached, requests a welfare check through 911 / law enforcement. 4.3 Camp-visit precautions • Announce yourselves on approach — who you are and why you're there. Never startle people. • Ask permission before entering someone's space. A tent is someone's home — treat it like one. • Keep exits in view. Don't let yourselves get boxed in. • Don't move or touch anyone's belongings. • Leave valuables locked in the vehicle. Carry a charged phone and the team first-aid and naloxone kit. 4.4 When a scene feels unsafe • Trust your gut and leave — immediately, calmly, together. No equipment, no appointment, and no pride is worth a life. • Regroup at the agreed rally point. The Field Team Lead decides whether to return, reroute, or end the outing. • Report what happened to the lead the same day — unsafe scenes get documented like any other incident (Section 10). 4.5 Joint runs with law enforcement • Joint operations are arranged through the CEO or designee — staff never freelance joint runs. • On a joint run, officers own scene safety; peer staff own engagement. Staff do not enter an area officers have secured until officers say it is clear. Shelter Security (takes effect when the shelter opens) 5.1 Entry, exit & sign-in • Every person entering or leaving signs in and out at the front desk, every time. No exceptions. • Staff offices, medication storage, and utility rooms stay locked. Members do not enter secured areas unaccompanied. 5.2 Curfew • Curfew is 9:00 PM (adjustable by the CEO as operations require). Members with verified work schedules may receive a written curfew exception from the Shift Lead. • A member arriving after curfew without an approved exception may be denied re-entry for that night — staff will offer an alternative referral (a partner shelter, 211, or transportation to a safe location) rather than leaving the person with nowhere to go. 5.3 Guests • Guests enter by staff permission only, sign in at the front desk, remain in common areas, and leave by 8:00 PM. No overnight guests. 5.4 Prohibited items & zero tolerance • The following result in immediate removal and, where appropriate, notification of law enforcement: any act of violence or credible threat of violence toward any person; possession of a weapon of any kind on shelter property; theft from members, staff, volunteers, or the shelter. There is no warning step for these. • Clean and sober property: no drugs or alcohol on shelter property — no exceptions. A member found using on the property must leave; struggling with addiction never disqualifies a person from our care, and staff will offer a treatment referral on the way out. Prescription medications are declared at intake and stored securely by staff. 5.5 Searches & bag checks • Staff may conduct bag checks at entry and when there is reasonable concern about prohibited items — respectfully, with dignity safeguards: a staff member of the same gender as the person being checked, in a private area, with a second staff member present as witness. • The person is told plainly what is happening and why, before it happens. A person who refuses a search is denied entry — that is the only consequence of refusal. No one is searched by force. Volunteers never conduct searches alone. • Prohibited items found are secured by staff and returned at departure — except weapons and illegal substances, which are turned over to law enforcement. 5.6 Nighttime procedures • The Shift Lead walks the house at lights-out: doors secured, common areas clear, quiet hours observed. Members are told at intake who to wake, and for what, during the night. Threat & Violence Response When danger is real and present — in the field or in the shelter — every person follows the same three steps, in order: 1. Get away if you can. Leave the area immediately. Do not wait for permission. 2. Get out of sight and stay quiet if you can't get away. Lock or barricade doors where possible. Silence phones. 3. As a last resort, protect yourself. Only to stop immediate physical harm, using no more force than necessary, until help arrives. 6.1 When to call 911 vs. the non-emergency line • Call 911 immediately for: weapons, active violence, serious injury, or any imminent threat to life. • Non-emergency line for: crimes discovered after the fact, trespassers who have already left, suspicious activity not currently in progress. 6.2 Protecting each other • Move people away from danger. Account for your buddy or partner — no one gets left behind in a crisis if you can safely help them. • Do not re-enter an unsafe area until law enforcement or the lead says it is clear. • Afterward: check on everyone involved, including witnesses. Complete the incident report the same day (Section 10). Follow up with affected members within 24 hours — a crisis does not end when the shouting stops. 7. Weapons Policy • Staff and volunteers do not carry weapons of any kind while performing company duties — no firearms, no knives carried as weapons, no other weapons. Period. • If you see a weapon: do not touch it and do not confront the person. Leave the area, call 911, and tell your lead. At the shelter: clear the immediate area, call 911, and let the Shift Lead secure the scene without touching the weapon. • Weapons found during a search are secured by staff and turned over to law enforcement — never returned on property. Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 8. Coordination with Law Enforcement & Partner Agencies Centipede works with law enforcement — as partners in safety, never as punishment. Our standing relationships include the Lodi Police Department (Chief Ricardo Garcia), the Stockton Police Department (Chief Stanley McFadden), the San Joaquin County Probation Department, and our community partners. This section is how that partnership works day to day. 8.1 Who calls • Operational needs (a scene right now): the Shift Lead at the shelter, or the Field Team Lead in the field, calls 911 or the agency's non-emergency line directly. • Agency-level coordination (planning joint outreach, ongoing issues, relationship matters): the CEO or designee — no one else commits the company to joint operations. 8.2 What information may be shared • In the moment, share what is needed to protect life and safety — descriptions, locations, immediate risks. • Client case details stay private. Member information is shared with law enforcement only to prevent imminent serious harm, as required by law (including suspected abuse or neglect reporting), or with the member's consent. A badge does not override confidentiality. 8.3 How joint operations are requested • Joint outreach is requested through the CEO or designee to the agency liaison, documented in advance where possible, with a debrief and written record afterward. • Staff never freelance: no independent “operations” with officers outside company coordination. 9. Medical Emergencies • Call 911 immediately for: overdose, chest pain, seizure, serious injury, trouble breathing. • Centipede is a non-clinical, peer-based program. Staff do not diagnose, treat, or provide clinical care — they recognize, respond, and refer. • Naloxone: trained staff may administer it when available and an opioid overdose is suspected. • Suicide crisis: call or text 988. Take every statement seriously. Stay with the person — do not leave them alone — until professional help arrives. • Mandatory reporting: suspected abuse or neglect of a child, elder, or dependent adult is reported as required by California law. Reporting & Documentation • Every incident gets written up the same day — conflict, injury, search, removal, medical or mental- health crisis, law-enforcement contact, and field near-misses — by the Shift Lead or Field Team Lead. • The CEO reviews every serious incident report within 24 hours and is notified immediately — by phone, any hour — of: any 911 call, any weapon, any injury requiring medical care, any removal, and any media presence. • Reports are factual: what happened, who was involved, what was done. No opinions, no diagnoses, no clinical language. • Reports are filed securely with access limited to authorized staff. Member information in reports is confidential, disclosed only as required by law, to prevent imminent serious harm, or to report suspected abuse or neglect. 11. Training & Policy Review 11.1 Training • Before a first solo shift, every staff member completes: this policy, de-escalation basics, the Mental Health Crisis Response Guide, incident-reporting procedure, confidentiality rules, and naloxone training where kits are issued. • Volunteers complete an orientation version before serving. • All staff complete an annual refresher. 11.2 Review cycle • This policy is reviewed by the Board of Directors at least annually, and after any serious incident that reveals a gap. • The CEO may issue interim safety updates between reviews; all interim updates are reported to the Board at its next meeting. • Effective dates: field-outreach provisions take effect on board adoption; shelter provisions take effect when the shelter opens. Consequences, delivered with dignity. Violations are addressed progressively — a private conversation first, then defined removal periods with written notice and the right to appeal — except the zero-tolerance items (violence, credible threats, weapons, theft), which mean immediate removal. And no one is ever removed into the night without staff offering an alternative referral where possible: a partner shelter, 211, or transportation somewhere safe. Safety does not end at our door.
