The Rule, Stated Plainly When anyone asks for a change within this company — a change to a policy, a program, an authorization, a role, or a standing practice — the change does not happen right away. It is recorded on the day it is asked, and it processes for about thirty (30) days before it takes effect. That is how this company works, and this policy states it company-wide so that everyone — the Board, employees, authorized users, and the community the company serves — knows it in advance: a request is the beginning of a change, not the change itself. 2. How a Change Processes • Day one — the request is recorded. Who asked, what was asked, and the date are entered in the company's records the day the request is made. Nothing about the request is treated as decided until it is recorded. • The processing period — about thirty days. During processing, the change is checked against the company's standing rules, its records, and its obligations; conflicts are flagged to the Chief Executive Officer; and the documents the change touches are prepared. The period runs about thirty days — long enough for the change to get through on its own, in order, without being rushed past a check that exists to protect the company and the people it serves. The change takes effect on its effective date — the day the processing completes — and is announced to the people it affects when it takes effect. Until that date, the company's existing rules and authorizations stand exactly as they were. 3. Who This Covers This rule covers change requests from anyone: employees, volunteers, board members, authorized users of company systems, partner organizations, and members of the community. It also covers a change the Chief Executive Officer directs on another person's request. A change the Chief Executive Officer makes to his own direction of the company, in writing, takes effect as he states in that writing. 4. Exceptions • A different date, in writing. The Chief Executive Officer may set a shorter or longer processing period for a named change, in writing. The written date governs. • Safety and legal deadlines. Anything bearing on a person's immediate safety, and anything a law or a court sets a deadline for, is handled at once. The thirty-day processing then applies to the standing change that follows, not to the urgent act itself. • Corrections are not changes. Correcting a plain error in a record — a misspelled name, a wrong date — is a correction, made when it is found and noted as a correction. It is not a change request and does not wait. 5. First Application The first change recorded under this policy is the identification of the authorized users of Jarvis under the Policy on Jarvis (CPP-POL-2026-001, Section 4.6), noted November 12, 2026. Under this policy, that authorized use becomes available on December 12, 2026, unless the Chief Executive Officer sets a different date in writing.
