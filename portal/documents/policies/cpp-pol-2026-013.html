Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 Issued by Levi Spears, Chief Executive Officer Member Public Relations Policy 1 MEMBER HANDBOOK CEO: LEVI SPEARS Welcome to Centipede You are a member of a community built on peer support, recovery, safety, and respect. You represent yourself first. When you participate in Centipede activities or identify yourself as a member, your choices can also affect other members and the public's trust in our work. Our promise: Centipede will treat you with dignity. You will not lose access to services merely because you decline an interview, photograph, testimonial, social media post, or other publicity activity. Purpose of this handbook This handbook explains the public-relations expectations for clients and members. It applies during Centipede programs, outreach, meetings, events, transportation, partner activities, interviews, and online activity that identifies or appears to represent Centipede. Your rights • To be treated fairly and without harassment, discrimination, humiliation, or retaliation. • To keep your personal story private or share only the parts you choose. • To ask questions before signing any consent or release. • To withdraw a photo or story authorization as allowed by the signed form. • To report a concern and receive a respectful response. • To request reasonable help understanding this handbook. Your responsibilities • Respect other members' privacy, boundaries, property, and recovery. • Be truthful about your role and do not claim to speak for Centipede without permission. • Follow safety directions from Centipede staff during activities and outreach. • Use communication channels responsibly and report serious concerns promptly. Important: This handbook does not replace Centipede's service-consent, privacy, photograph- authorization, or release-of-information forms. Those signed forms control the specific permissions they cover. Official representative: A person specifically authorized by the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) or the CEO's designee to speak or act for Centipede. Plain-language rule: If you are not sure whether you can share, post, photograph, record, or speak for Centipede, pause and ask a staff member first. Who to contact Questions, approvals, and concerns may be directed to Centipede staff or the CEO using the contact information on the cover and in the footer of this handbook. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC 4 PRIVACY & CONFIDENTIALITY Protect people's information Your story belongs to you. Other members' stories belong to them. Do not repeat, post, record, or disclose another person's information without their clear permission. Do protect • Names, faces, phone numbers, addresses, birth dates, identification numbers, and contact details. • Health, mental-health, substance-use, recovery, medication, housing, and benefit information. • Legal, probation, court, immigration, and law-enforcement information. • Details learned in peer groups, transportation, outreach, encampments, offices, or partner settings. • Exact locations that could expose someone to harassment, theft, displacement, or danger. Do not • Take screenshots of private messages and post or forward them. • Repeat group discussions outside the group. • Tag, identify, or check in another member without permission. • Photograph paperwork, sign-in sheets, identification, medication, or computer screens. • Ask staff to confirm whether a person receives services. Centipede's information-sharing rule: Centipede does not share a member's information merely because someone asks. Information is handled under Centipede's privacy procedures, signed release- of-information forms, and applicable law. A release should identify what may be shared, with whom, for what purpose, and for how long. Limits and urgent safety Privacy is not a promise to hide an immediate danger, suspected abuse, or another situation that law or policy requires staff to address. If you are unsure, speak privately with staff rather than posting or contacting outside parties yourself. When in doubt: Stop. Do not share. Ask a staff member. Member Public Relations Policy 5 MEDIA & PUBLIC COMMUNICATION Speak for yourself, not for the organization You may share your own experience and opinions. Only a person authorized by the CEO or the CEO's designee may give an official statement, approve an interview, or make commitments for Centipede. If a reporter asks Never claim 1 You may decline to answer. • That you are a Centipede employee, clinician, officer, attorney, police officer, probation 2 Say, “I can speak only for myself.” officer, or spokesperson unless that role is 3 Do not discuss another member. formally yours. 4 Refer official questions to Centipede staff or • That Centipede guarantees housing, benefits, the CEO. treatment, money, court outcomes, or public policy changes. 5 Tell staff about the request promptly. • That a personal view is Centipede's official position. Safe ways to share your story • Decide in advance what topics are private. • Avoid naming other people or revealing details that identify them. • Ask where and for how long the story will appear. • Ask whether the interview is recorded, livestreamed, or editable. • Request a staff support person if you want one. • Do not sign a release you do not understand; ask for an explanation or time to review it. No pressure: Participation in publicity must be voluntary. A member may say no, stop an interview, or decline a testimonial without losing Centipede services. Correcting public information Send possible errors, impersonation, or misuse of Centipede's name or logo to staff. Do not start an online argument on behalf of Centipede. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC 6 SOCIAL MEDIA Keep personal and official communication clear You may • Share your own experience in your own words. • Say you are a member or participant if you choose. • Share a Centipede post from an official account without changing its meaning. • Express a personal opinion when you make clear it is your own. You may not • Create an account, page, group, fundraiser, event, or advertisement that appears official without written approval. • Use Centipede's logo, letterhead, forms, staff titles, or partner names to imply authorization. • Post another person's image, story, documents, location, messages, or service information without permission. • Livestream from a private meeting, service appointment, vehicle, encampment, or outreach activity without staff authorization and required consent. • Harass, threaten, dox, impersonate, or deliberately spread false information about members, staff, volunteers, partners, or the organization. • Solicit money, gifts, rides, housing, medication, drugs, or personal favors from members through a Centipede- related channel. Suggested disclosure: “I am speaking for myself. My views are not an official statement from Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC.” Before you post □ Is it true and based on what I personally know? □ Is it mine to share? □ Could a name, face, location, or detail identify someone? □ Am I calm enough to post respectfully? □ Could readers reasonably think this is an official Centipede statement? □ Do I have written approval to use the logo or other branded materials? Remember: Deleting a post may not remove screenshots or copies. Pause before posting. Member Public Relations Policy 7 PHOTOS, VIDEO & STORIES Consent must be informed and specific No member is required to be photographed, recorded, interviewed, or featured. Participation in services is separate from permission to use a person's image or story. Before capturing or using content 1 Explain the purpose. State who is collecting the content and why. 2 Explain where it may appear. Examples include a report, website, social media, news story, grant material, or training. 3 Confirm the choice is voluntary. No pressure, reward, threat, or loss of service may be tied to consent. 4 Use the correct form. Obtain the required signed photograph or media authorization before identifiable use. 5 Respect limits. Follow all choices about face visibility, name use, location, subject matter, and duration. Special care in encampments and outreach settings • Do not photograph a tent, vehicle, sleeping area, property, document, or identifying landmark without permission. • Do not assume that being in public is consent to become Centipede content. • Avoid images of people in crisis, receiving care, using substances, sleeping, partially dressed, or in a humiliating or unsafe situation. • Do not stage, pressure, or trade services for a testimonial. • Stop recording when asked and notify staff if consent is withdrawn. Children and protected adults: Do not photograph, record, interview, or publish content involving a minor or a person unable to provide informed consent unless Centipede has confirmed all required permissions. Member-created content A member using a personal device is still responsible for privacy and consent. Staff authorization may be required during programs, in vehicles, on partner property, or at outreach locations. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC 8 OUTREACH & PARTNERS Be clear about roles in the field Centipede may coordinate outreach with community partners, including the Stockton Police Department and the San Joaquin County Probation Department. Partnership does not make a member an officer, probation employee, clinician, or official spokesperson. At outreach activities and public events • Check in with the designated Centipede lead and follow the safety briefing. • Wear only approved identification or clothing and return borrowed materials. • Introduce yourself accurately as a member or participant, not as staff unless employed in that role. • Let each partner explain its own authority, rules, and services. • Do not promise that speaking with Centipede will prevent arrest, probation action, citation, removal, hospitalization, or other official action. • Do not collect records, signatures, photos, money, medication, or property unless staff has assigned and trained you for that task. • Do not interfere with law enforcement, probation activity, emergency response, clinical care, or another agency's work. • Move to a safe area and notify staff if a scene becomes unstable. Community boundaries Acceptable Not acceptable • Offer peer encouragement. • Diagnose or give medical advice. • Share approved service information. • Give legal or probation advice. • Help someone connect with staff. • Transport someone without approval. • Describe your own recovery experience. • Handle medication or money. • Ask before entering personal space. • Use partner names to pressure anyone. Voluntary engagement: Peer support should be offered respectfully. Unless an authorized agency states otherwise under its own lawful authority, a person may decline to speak with Centipede or accept services. After an activity Return identification and materials, report safety or privacy concerns, and share program details only through approved channels. Do not post names, photos, locations, or incident details from the field. Member Public Relations Policy 9 CONCERNS & RESPONSES Problems should be reported safely and handled fairly How to raise a concern 1 If there is immediate danger, move to safety and contact emergency services. 2 Tell a Centipede staff member or the CEO as soon as reasonably possible. 3 Describe what happened, when and where it happened, who was involved, and any immediate safety needs. 4 Preserve relevant messages or posts without reposting confidential content publicly. 5 Ask for help making a written complaint if needed. No retaliation Centipede prohibits retaliation against a person who makes a good-faith report, asks a question, declines publicity, requests privacy, or participates in a review. Knowingly false accusations or deliberate misuse of the complaint process may be addressed separately. How Centipede may respond Centipede will consider safety, seriousness, intent, impact, prior incidents, and the member's support needs. Responses may include coaching, clarification, a restorative conversation when appropriate, a written agreement, limits on specific activities, removal from an event, temporary suspension, or termination from a program when necessary. Proportionate response: A service or participation limit should be connected to the behavior and safety risk. When feasible, Centipede will explain the reason, expected correction, duration, and review path. Urgent conduct Threats, violence, weapons, exploitation, stalking, severe harassment, unauthorized disclosure of highly sensitive information, or conduct creating immediate danger may require removal, emergency response, partner notification, or other action allowed by policy and law. Review request A member may ask the CEO or designee to review a non-emergency restriction or decision. The request should explain what the member believes was misunderstood and what resolution is requested.
