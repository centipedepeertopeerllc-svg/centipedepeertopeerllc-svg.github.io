CENTIPEDE PEER TO PEER, LLC CPP-POL-2026-047 — Electronic Signatures Policy Issued November 18, 2026, by direction of the Chief Executive Officer. 1. PURPOSE. Centipede's clients sign their papers in person, in conversation with the staff member working with them — at intake, and whenever a program form needs a signature after intake. This policy sets the company's standard for taking those signatures electronically: how a signature is captured, what is recorded with it, and where the signed record lives, so that a signature taken on a screen carries the same weight, and is kept with the same care, as a signature taken with a pen. The company built its own signature capture into its own systems for this purpose; no outside signature service is used, and no client information is sent to any outside service to be signed. 2. SCOPE. This policy applies to every client signature and every staff signature on a company form, in every program — including every client form used by the company's twelve programs, from the Intake & Participation Agreement and the Release of Information to the program forms of Homeless Outreach, the Homeless Shelter, Justice Support, and the rest. It covers both places the company takes electronic signatures: (a) the signature steps inside the Client Dashboard intake flow, and (b) the standalone Sign a Form page in the Company Portal, which exists for any company form signed outside intake. It works alongside the Intake Before Membership Policy (CPP-POL-2026-043), which states what must be signed at intake, and the Program Binders and Dashboard Records Policy (CPP-POL-2026-044), which states where the official record lives. 3. LEGAL BASIS. [This policy is marked for counsel review.] California's Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA), Civil Code section 1633.1 and following, provides that a signature may not be denied legal effect solely because it is in electronic form, and that where a law requires a signature, an electronic signature satisfies the law when the parties have agreed to transact electronically. The federal ESIGN Act, 15 U.S.C. section 7001 and following, provides the same rule for transactions in or affecting interstate commerce: an electronic signature has the same legal effect as a handwritten one where the signer has consented to sign electronically. This policy is built on those two foundations, held the way the statutes hold them: consent first, the signing done by the signer himself in the presence of a witness, and a complete record of the signing kept and retrievable. The company states this basis plainly so that any reader — a client, an auditor, a court — can see exactly what the company's electronic signatures rest on. 4. CONSENT BEFORE SIGNING. No electronic signature is taken until the signer has agreed to sign electronically. Before the signature pad is offered, the signer confirms, in the signing flow itself: "I agree to sign this document electronically and understand my electronic signature has the same effect as a handwritten one." That confirmation is part of every signing record. A signer who does not agree signs on paper instead, under Section 7; the electronic flow is never pressed on anyone. Consent under this policy is consent to the signing in front of the signer — each form, each time — and is never treated as a blanket consent to future signings. 5. HOW SIGNING IS DONE. Electronic signing at Centipede is done in person, on a company or staff device, with a staff member present throughout. The order of the signing is fixed: (1) the signer is shown the form itself and given the time to read it, with staff answering questions in plain language; (2) the signer prints his or her full name; (3) the signer confirms the consent statement of Section 4; (4) the signer signs in the signature pad, with a finger, a stylus, or a mouse, and may clear and redo the signature until it is his or her own; (5) the staff member present enters his or her own name as the staff member present; and (6) the system stamps the signing with the date and time automatically. A staff member never signs for another person, never signs a blank or incomplete signing, and never completes a signing for a person who is not present. 6. WHAT IS RECORDED WITH EVERY SIGNATURE. Every electronic signing records, together and inseparably: the name of the form signed; the signer's printed full name; the signer's signature, captured as an image of what the signer drew; the date and time of the signing, stamped automatically by the system; and the name of the staff member present. A signing that is missing any of these is not a completed signing under this policy: it is voided, the gap is noted in the client's record, and the signing is done again properly. 7. PAPER ALTERNATIVE — ALWAYS AVAILABLE. Any person may sign any company form on paper instead of electronically, at any time, for any reason or no reason. No client is ever required to sign electronically. Choosing paper is never questioned, never noted against the person, and never delays or conditions any service. A paper signing is filed the same way as an electronic one — into the client's locked binder — and carries exactly the same standing. Staff offer the paper alternative plainly whenever a signer hesitates, cannot use the device, or asks. 8. DECLINES. A person may decline to sign a form altogether, on paper or electronically. When that happens, the staff member records the decline — the form, the date, and the staff member's name — in the client's record, and the matter is handled exactly as the form's own rules provide. A decline is a fact recorded, never a mark against the client: it is never held against the client, never used to deny a service the client is otherwise eligible for, and never argued with in the moment. Where a form is one the company's rules require before a service begins — the intake documents under CPP-POL-2026-043 are the standing example — the consequence is the one those rules state, explained to the person plainly and without pressure. 9. FORMS NOT YET IN USE. A form marked DRAFT, or otherwise not yet in use, is not signed — electronically or on paper — until the company puts the form in use. The signing systems carry those forms labeled "(DRAFT — not yet in use)" so the state of the form is visible at the moment of signing, and the standalone Sign a Form page refuses a completed signing on a form so marked. This section applies today to the Justice Support Participant Accountability Agreement (draft, not yet in use) and to the Super Hero Program forms (drafts, not in use, pending board approval on November 27, 2026): they are listed so staff can see the full form inventory, and they become signable only when the company puts them in use. 10. RELEASES OF INFORMATION. A Release of Information signed electronically under this policy is a signed Release of Information for every company purpose. The standing disclosure rule is unchanged and governs every release, however signed: client information leaves this company only on (1) a signed client Release of Information or (2) a judge-signed court order. A subpoena alone is not a court order. An electronic signature on a Release of Information carries the same weight as a handwritten one, and a release that was declined, expired, or revoked is honored exactly as a paper one is. 11. THE OFFICIAL RECORD. The signed record produced by a signing is printed and filed into the client's locked binder — or, for a staff signing, into the employee's HR file — and the binder copy is the official record, under CPP-POL-2026-044. The copy held on the device that took the signing is a working copy: it is kept so the record can be reprinted if a print is lost before filing, and it is never treated as the record itself. Signed records are retained with the client's file under the company's record-keeping rules, and are produced, when they must be produced, from the binder. 12. INTEGRITY AND STAFF RESPONSIBILITIES. The staff member present is the witness to the signing and is responsible for its regularity: the right form, the right signer, the consent confirmed, the signature the signer's own, and the printed record filed the same day. Any irregularity — a signing taken on the wrong form, a signature that is not the signer's, a record that cannot be printed — is reported to the Chief Executive Officer the same day and corrected on the record, with the correction dated and the original entry never rewritten silently. Suspected misuse of the signing systems is reported to the CEO the same day it is seen. 13. COUNSEL REVIEW. Sections 3 and 10 of this policy carry legal weight beyond company practice, and this policy is marked for counsel review at the company's next counsel touchpoint, the same treatment CPP-POL-2026-045 gives its Section 9. The rest of this policy states the company's own working standard — consent, presence, a complete record, a paper alternative always open, and the binder as the official record — and stands on the company's own authority to set it. 14. MONEY AND SCOPE. This policy authorizes no purchase. The signature capture it governs is built into the company's own Portal and dashboards; no outside electronic-signature service is engaged under this policy, and no client data is sent outside the company's systems for signing. Nothing in this policy makes any Centipede service clinical, and nothing in it states or implies nonprofit or charitable status: Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a California for-profit limited liability company.
