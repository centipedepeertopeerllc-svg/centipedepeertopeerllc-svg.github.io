Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 1. Purpose 1.1 The Centipede shelter is a clean and sober house. This policy exists for one reason: to protect every person's safety and recovery — including yours. 1.2 Drug and alcohol testing is a safety tool, not a weapon. It is never used to shame, embarrass, or punish anyone. It gives us clear information so we can respond fairly and keep the house safe for everybody inside it. 1.3 This policy works together with the Letter of Expectations and the Shelter Security Policy (CSP-SEC-001). Where they overlap, the safety of the house governs. 2. The Honor Pledge 2.1 Every member gives their word when signing this agreement: (a) No drugs or alcohol on shelter property — no using, no possessing, no bringing anything onto the grounds. (b) If I slip, I will come forward honestly and early, before anyone has to ask. (c) I will ask for help when I feel myself slipping, and I will accept the help that is offered. 2.2 Your word matters here. A slip that you report yourself — told honestly, before any test or incident — is met with help: peer support, a treatment referral, and a plan. It is not met with removal from the house. 2.3 Honor cuts both ways. What you tell us in confidence stays in confidence, under Section 5. Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 3. When Testing Occurs 3.1 We do not do random mass testing. Nobody is ever tested without a reason. 3.2 A test may be requested when: (a) Staff observe behavior suggesting impairment — such as slurred speech, unsteady movement, confusion, or the odor of alcohol or drugs; (b) An incident occurs on the property and impairment may be a factor; (c) A member returns from an unexplained overnight absence and staff have reasonable concern. 3.3 The staff member will tell you plainly why a test is being requested. You have the right to know the reason. 4. How Testing Works 4.1 Testing is done privately and with dignity — never in front of other members, never as a spectacle. 4.2 The procedure follows the same dignity safeguards as our other safety practices: a private setting, a same-gender staff member, and a second staff member aware and nearby. 4.3 We use an approved screening method, administered according to its instructions by trained staff. 4.4 If you believe a result is wrong, you may request one retest, done promptly under the same dignified conditions. The retest result stands. 4.5 Refusing a requested test is your choice — but the refusal will be documented, and it is treated the same as a confirmed positive under Section 6. We state this plainly so there are never any surprises. Longview Ave., Stockton, CA 95207 CALMHSA DRIVEN 5. Confidentiality 5.1 Test results are seen only by you, the shift lead, and the CEO. They are never posted, never announced, and never gossiped about. 5.2 Results are stored with your member records and kept private under our privacy practices. 5.3 Any staff member who breaks this confidence faces discipline, up to removal from their role. Your dignity is not optional here. 6. Consequences 6.1 You come to us first. If you self-report a slip before any test or incident, you meet help — not punishment. We sit down together, build a plan, connect you to treatment or detox if you want it, and increase peer-support check-ins. You keep your bed. 6.2 Confirmed use on the property — by test result or by direct observation — means what the Letter of Expectations already told you: using on the property means leaving the property. You will be asked to leave the same day. 6.3 Leaving is not abandonment. Before you go, we give you a warm referral — to treatment, detox, or another safe placement — and our phone number. When you are ready to live clean and sober in community again, our door is open. Come back, and we start fresh. 6.4 No one is ever put out into the night without the offer of an alternative safe referral. 7. Prescription Medications 7.1 Tell us about your prescriptions at intake. Keep them as your doctor directed, stored safely. 7.2 A legitimate prescription, taken as prescribed, is never held against you and never counts as a violation. If a screening reflects your prescribed medication, that is the end of it. 7.3 If you are unsure whether something you take could affect a screening, ask us. Asking is always safe.
