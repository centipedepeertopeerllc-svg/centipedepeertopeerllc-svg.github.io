Rules of the Ride 2 6. Adoption 2 1. Purpose Our shelter van exists for one reason: getting members to the health screening they must complete to stay with us. This policy draws a clear line so the van serves that purpose — fairly, for everyone, with no confusion and no favoritism. 2. The Required Health Screening Every person staying in the shelter must complete a chest x-ray (tuberculosis screening). This is a requirement of the stay, not an option. People sleep side by side in a shelter. One undetected case of tuberculosis can put the whole house at risk — members, staff, volunteers, and the neighbors we serve. The chest x-ray protects everyone under our roof, and it protects you: if something shows up, we will help you get care right away. There is no shame in it. It is simply how a responsible house takes care of its people. The screening must be completed before or at the start of your stay. Staff will schedule it with you at check-in. 3. What Transportation Covers Shelter transportation covers one thing: the ride to and from your required chest x-ray screening appointment. • Staff schedule the appointment and the ride with you. • The ride is round-trip: we take you there, we bring you back. • Keep the appointment. A missed screening appointment delays your stay and counts against you. What Transportation Does Not Cover Everything else is the member’s responsibility: job interviews, personal appointments, shopping, visits, and day-to-day travel. The shelter van is not a taxi service, and there are no joyrides. Any transportation beyond the required screening ride requires the personal approval of the Chief Executive Officer — no exceptions, no matter who asks. Where the check-in materials mention staff help getting to interviews, that help is arranged case by case, subject to availability, and this policy governs: the CEO decides. 5. Rules of the Ride • Be on time and ready when the ride is scheduled. • Be sober and respectful in the vehicle — the ride is a privilege of the stay. • Seatbelts on. No smoking, eating, or drinking in the van. • Missed appointments without a real emergency are documented and may affect your stay. 6. Adoption This policy is issued as CSP-TR-001, dated October 2, 2026. It takes effect upon the opening of the Centipede shelter and only after formal adoption by the Board of Directors. It works alongside the Check-In Form & Stay Agreement (CSP-CI-001) and the Shelter Security Policy (CSP-SEC-001); where they overlap on safety, the Security Policy governs. Levi A.
