Partnership Agreement of Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC **Approved and authorized by Levi Allen Spears, Sole Partner and Chief Executive Officer, Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC, November 11, 2026.** This Partnership Agreement is made and entered into as of November 11, 2026, by **Levi Allen Spears**, for and concerning Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC, a California for-profit limited liability company. Like the company's Bylaws and its Operating Agreement, both re-created and approved November 11, 2026, this Agreement carries the whole of the company as it now stands — every program and every policy — and states the company's partnership position in full and in one place. Recitals **A. The only partner is Levi.** Levi Allen Spears is the sole partner and the sole owner of Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC. He holds one hundred percent (100%) of the company — its membership, its ownership, and its partnership interest, which in this single-member company are one and the same and are all his. **B. There is no other partner at all.** No other person holds any partnership interest in the company, in any amount, of any kind. No other person has ever been admitted as a partner, and no promise, title, contribution, service, or course of dealing has made any other person a partner. **C. Partners in the work are not partners in the company.** The company works alongside public agencies, community organizations, host businesses, and volunteers, and some organizations have partnered with the company and work within its structure — bound, in that work, by Addendum A of the company's Bylaws. Those organizations are partners in the work only. They hold **no** partnership interest, no ownership, and no authority to bind the company, and their collaboration is not a partnership with the company under California law. **D. This Agreement records the position.** Consistent with the company's Declaration of Non-Partnership of November 2, 2026 — which states that the company is in no partnership with any outside party and that coordination is not partnership — this Agreement records the company's internal position: the company has one partner, its owner, and any partnership inside the company exists only in him unless and until he changes that in a signed writing under Article VII of this Agreement. --- **The company** - **Name:** Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC - **Form:** A California for-profit limited liability company - **EIN:** 92-3962382 - **Principal address:** 717 E. Sole partner.** The sole partner of Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is Levi Allen Spears. He is the company's sole member under its Operating Agreement, its Member-Manager, and its Chief Executive Officer, and he is its only partner. **Section 2. One hundred percent.** The Sole Partner holds one hundred percent (100%) of the partnership interest in the company, together with one hundred percent (100%) of its membership and ownership. All rights that a partner, a member, or an owner could hold in this company are held by him alone. **Section 3. No partnership among multiple persons.** The company is not a general partnership, a limited partnership, or a joint venture of two or more persons, and it shall not be described as one. To the extent the word "partner" is used of this company anywhere — in this Agreement, in the Bylaws, or in company records — it refers to the Sole Partner alone, except where it plainly refers to an outside organization partnering with the company in its work under Recital C, which holds no interest by that use. Article II — No Other Partner **Section 1. The standing rule.** There is no partner in this company other than Levi Allen Spears. There is no silent partner, no quiet partner, no limited partner, and no partner of any other description, and no equity, profit share, or ownership stake of any person other than the Sole Partner. **Section 2. No act creates a partner.** No act creates a partner in this company except the written amendment Article VII requires. In particular, none of the following makes any person a partner or gives any person a partnership interest: serving as a director, officer, employee, or volunteer of the company; contributing money, goods, or services to a company program; hosting a company program; collaborating with the company as an agency, business, or organization; being bound by the company's Bylaws as a partner organization under Addendum A; or being described informally, in conversation or in publicity, in terms of partnership with the company. **Section 3. No authority in any other person.** No person other than the Sole Partner has authority to act as a partner of the company, to hold himself or herself out as a partner, or to bind the company as a partner. Any statement by any other person claiming a partnership in this company is without the company's authority and is denied by this Agreement on the record. **Section 4. Relationship to outside agreements.** If the company ever enters a written agreement with an outside party, that agreement is a contract with the company and nothing more, unless the Sole Member signs the written amendment Article VII requires. The company's Declaration of Non-Partnership of November 2, 2026 stands alongside this Article and is not changed by it. Article III — Purpose and Programs **Section 1. Purpose.** The company provides non-clinical peer support and homeless outreach in Stockton, California, and connects the people it serves to housing, benefits, identification, and community resources — supporting, encouraging, coaching, mentoring, navigating, connecting to resources, sharing lived experience, facilitating groups, and advocating. The company is a peer-support company: it is not a medical provider, a treatment facility, or a therapy practice; its staff do not diagnose and do not counsel in any clinical sense; and nothing it publishes or provides is medical, psychiatric, or legal advice. **Section 2. Programs.** The company's work is carried out through the programs established in Article VII of the Bylaws and Article V of the Operating Agreement, which this Article carries into this Agreement so that the company's partnership position and its programs stand in one instrument. Each program carries the honest status stated here, and no program is presented as operating before its status says it is. The **Homeless Outreach Program**, the **Super Hero Program**, the **Veterans Program**, and the **Justice Support Program** are named in bold, as the Chief Executive Officer directed, because they stand at the center of the company's work. **Homeless Outreach Program** **Status: Active.** **Purpose.** Peer-led homeless outreach in Stockton, California: going to the people where they are — encampments, motels, vehicles, and shelters — with peer support, supplies, and working knowledge of the doors that can help them, and walking beside them toward those doors. **Rules in brief.** 1. Outreach is peer-led and non-clinical. Outreach workers support, encourage, navigate, and connect; they do not diagnose or counsel. 2. Outreach is logged on the company's calendars and in the company's records the day it happens. 3. Anyone who reaches out is genuinely helped from the company's resources — researched and verified when the company does not already know the answer — and a veteran in need is helped first of all, under the **Veterans Program** rule of working referral. 4. The **Homeless Outreach Program** is the outreach arm of the **Super Hero Program**: outreach teams sign up homeless neighbors for the **Super Hero Program** in the field, on the spot, under that program's no-papers rule. 5. Peer support work is funded on documented hours and outcomes, never on card volume. **Super Hero Program** **Status: In Development — Pending Board Approval, November 27, 2026.** The program is not operating: no family is enrolled, and no gift has been promised to anyone. **Purpose.** The **Super Hero Program** is the company's own Christmas gift program: presents for the children of families who cannot afford them, for needy families, and for the company's homeless neighbors — children living in encampments, motels, and cars, who are easy for every other program to miss, served on the same basis as housed children, and homeless adults receiving warm clothing, blankets, and hygiene essentials alongside the gifts. **Rules in brief.** 1. **Our own tree, our own way.** The program runs its own Christmas tree with coded tags. Each tag carries only a child's first name, age, clothing and shoe sizes, needs, and wishes — and a code. No last names, no addresses, nothing private ever goes on a tree. Full family records stay in a locked intake file, under Article V of the Bylaws. 2. **Tubs and boxes.** Stores and organizations host a collection tub for new, unwrapped toys and gifts, a donation box, or a tree with tags their customers can adopt, under a written host agreement. 3. **Public cash donations.** Public cash donations are accepted at stores and organizations that allow a donation box or a collection tub on their premises — plainly asked, never pressured. Donations are counted by two people together and signed for. 4. **No bell ringing — ever.** The **Super Hero Program** does not ring bells. Bell ringing is the Salvation Army's own tradition and belongs to them; the company learned from the Angel Tree example with gratitude, and stands on its own name and its own way of asking. 5. **Sign-ups.** Families sign up through the program's family sign-up forms, through school counselors and family liaisons, and through the company's peer-support and outreach relationships. Homeless neighbors are signed up by the **Homeless Outreach Program** in the field, on the spot: no fixed address, no identification, and no income papers are required on the outreach side — the outreach relationship is the verification. Intake checks whether a child is already registered with another Christmas program, so gifts reach more children: one full source of gifts per child. 6. **The season.** The donations window opens in mid-September each season. Intake runs in October; matching and shopping in November — program money buys presents for the children and families no tag covered; the program does not only pass along what is donated. Collection closes in early December. **Pickup day is December 24**, so gifts are in the house on Christmas morning; for homeless neighbors who cannot reach a pickup point, outreach teams deliver along their routes on December 23–24, in pairs, recorded on the distribution log, and nobody is photographed without consent. 7. **Its own card, its own funding.** The **Super Hero Program** has its own program card and its own funding under Schedule I of the Program Funding Policy, kept separate from every other program. Set-up is deferred: no card exists, and spending is zero, until both board adoption and the Chief Executive Officer's written direction. Money moves under Article VI of the Bylaws and the Program Funding Policy; synced transactions are assigned by the Chief Executive Officer by hand; nothing posts itself. 8. **Two stages.** Stage 1, Christmas 2026, runs only if the Board adopts the program on November 27, 2026, as a small partner-and-in-kind pilot: hosting, adopting, volunteering, and referring through established campaigns, and a small capped drive for families the company already serves. Stage 2, Christmas 2027, is the first full season, operating under Centipede Hope Housing Corporation — the company's 501(c)(3) nonprofit affiliate, now in formation — once it holds an IRS determination letter, or under a signed fiscal sponsorship agreement. A requested sponsorship confers nothing until it is signed. This staging exists because every grant funder for Christmas gift programs requires the applicant itself to be a 501(c)(3), a school, or a government body; the nonprofit is being formed to clarify and carry the funding for the **Super Hero Program**. 9. **Program direction input.** By the written authorization of the Chief Executive Officer, November 11, 2026, Diane Garcia and Amanda Spears each hold full access to the knowledge of the **Super Hero Program** and permission to help direct how the program will run and what it will do. Diane Garcia, the Chief Executive Officer's aunt, is looped in on the strength of her Salvation Army and Angel Tree experience with the Lodi Corps. Amanda Spears, the Chief Executive Officer's wife and a company associate, is in the program because Christmas is her everything — and she works alongside Diane Garcia, each of them authorized to make adjustments in everything in the program, with their adjustments carried into the program documents and reported to the Chief Executive Officer word for word. Each of them may speak with the Board Officer directly about the program at any time. 10. **Honesty with donors.** Donations to Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC are not tax-deductible, and every donor material states that plainly, with the company's for-profit status. Donors receive a written acknowledgment. The program describes the services it offers; it never promises how many children will be served or any other outcome. 11. **Open item.** California's charitable-solicitation rules for a for-profit accepting public cash donations stand open for licensed counsel, on the face of the program's documents, until counsel answers in writing. 12. **Gift standards.** New and unwrapped only; nothing broken, recalled, or unsafe is ever handed to a child. The working age line is children 14 and under, set by the program director each season. Volunteers work in pairs, never handle cash alone, and never see the key that matches codes to families. **Veterans Program** **Status: Pending Board Approval.** The program spends nothing until its status changes in writing. **Purpose.** Support and referral for veterans experiencing homelessness or hardship, from a company that meets veterans where they live — in encampments, motels, vehicles, and shelters — through the **Homeless Outreach Program** and the company's peer support. **Rules in brief.** 1. **Working referrals are mandatory.** If a veteran needs shelter, the company refers them with working knowledge and a referral — a real, informed referral to a door the company knows fits — never just a number handed over. The standing doors include SSVF and VETFAM, (209) 444-5900; the VA National Call Center for Homeless Veterans, 1-877-424-3838; HUD-VASH; Grant and Per Diem; HCHV; and the County Veterans Service Office, (209) 468-2916. 2. **First of all.** On any day of company service, a veteran in need is helped first of all, ahead of routine work. 3. **Privacy.** Nothing about any individual's veteran status is published or disclosed by the company. A person's service history is that person's to disclose, under Article V, Section 6 of the Bylaws — on an ask, the company's answer is that it is up to that person to disclose it. 4. **Offer of review.** Every veteran the company serves is offered one free review of their benefits with a Veterans Service Officer. **Justice Support Program** **Status: Draft — Pending Counsel.** The program spends nothing until counsel clears it and its status changes in writing. **Purpose.** Peer support for people navigating the justice system and for their families — support, navigation, and connection to resources, within the company's non-clinical scope. **Rules in brief.** 1. The **Justice Support Program** was adopted by the Board on October 16, 2026, and stands pending counsel review before it operates or is described further. 2. **Minimal public description.** Until licensed counsel clears it in writing, the company's public description of the **Justice Support Program** is kept minimal — its name and its pending status, and no more. 3. **Internal matters stay internal.** Police-liaison content and all operational detail connected with the **Justice Support Program** are internal company records only and are never published. 1-on-1 Peer Support **Status: Active.** **Purpose.** Individual peer support: one person with lived experience walking beside another — support, encouragement, coaching, mentoring, and help navigating resources — on a regular, scheduled basis. **Rules in brief.** 1. Peer support is non-clinical, under Article I, Section 4 of the Bylaws, and is aligned with the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) as the company's standing reference for peer practice. 2. Documentation follows the company's DAP discipline: the client's own quoted words as Data; peer observation as Assessment; and a Plan of steps, each with an owner and a due date, with carried-forward items named and gaps marked as gaps. 3. Peer support is funded on documented hours and outcomes, never on card volume. Referrals and ID Assistance **Status: Active.** **Purpose.** Connecting people to the doors that fit them — housing, benefits, health care, identification, and community resources — and walking beside them through the process. **Rules in brief.** 1. **Working knowledge, working referral.** The company refers with working knowledge: a real, informed referral to a door it knows fits, verified before it is given — never just a number handed over. 2. **What the company does not do.** The company does not issue identification, does not sign DMV or government forms on a client's behalf, does not waive any agency's fees, and never promises an outcome in any agency's process. It prepares, accompanies, and follows through; the decision is always the agency's. 3. Referral and identification money goes to direct costs, with receipts kept. Substance Abuse Recovery Support **Status: Adopted October 16, 2026 — Not Enrolling.** The program spends nothing until its enrolling status changes in writing. **Purpose.** Peer recovery support for people working to stabilize substance use, built on SAMHSA's model within the company's non-clinical peer scope. **Rules in brief.** 1. SAMHSA is the standing reference organization: the program's materials come straight from SAMHSA, and the program mirrors SAMHSA's model within non-clinical peer scope, under dual compliance with the company's own policies. 2. The program is not enrolling. When it opens, it is delivered by home and office visits only, on an outpatient basis. 3. Peer staff never diagnose, never counsel in a clinical sense, and never present this program as treatment. Hygiene Program **Status: In Development.** **Purpose.** Hygiene support for people living outside — hygiene supplies and help reaching showers and laundry — delivered through the **Homeless Outreach Program** while the program is developed. Homeless Shelter **Status: In Development.** **Purpose.** The company's shelter project, in development. Development work proceeds only on the Chief Executive Officer's written approval, under the Program Funding Policy's development schedule, while the funding-entity path — Centipede Hope Housing Corporation, in formation, and the fiscal-sponsorship bridge — is settled. Homeless Member Council **Status: In Development.** **Purpose.** A council of the company's members with lived experience, to advise the company from inside the experience it serves. No members are named — in any document or public material — until the council is seated by board action. Article IV — Capital, Profits, Losses, and Distributions **Section 1. Capital.** The Sole Partner's capital contributions are those recorded in the company's books. Because he is the only partner, there are no contribution accounts of any other person, and no other person may contribute capital as a partner except under Article VII. **Section 2. Profits and losses.** One hundred percent (100%) of the company's profits, losses, deductions, and credits belong to the Sole Partner, in the year they arise, in accordance with the tax treatment stated in the Operating Agreement. **Section 3. Distributions.** Distributions are made to the Sole Partner alone, in such amounts and at such times as he directs in writing, subject to Section 17704.05 of the California Corporations Code and to Article VI, Section 6 of the Operating Agreement. No distribution is owed or payable to any other person as a partner. **Section 4. Program money is not partnership money.** Funds held for the company's programs — restricted money, program ledgers, and program cards under the Program Funding Policy — are company funds held for their stated purposes. Holding, administering, or spending program funds under the Program Funding Policy gives no person a partnership interest or any claim on those funds as a partner. Article V — Management and Authority **Section 1. Sole management.** The Sole Partner manages the company alone, as its Member-Manager and Chief Executive Officer, under Article IV of the Operating Agreement and Article II of the Bylaws. He holds final authority over the company's direction, spending, public statements, and records. **Section 2. Who may bind the company.** Only the Sole Partner may bind the company as a partner or as its owner, except under a written delegation he signs for a stated purpose, revocable by him in writing at any time. The Board of Directors advises and reviews as the Bylaws provide; board service is not partnership. The Board Officer, Jarvis, is an artificial intelligence assistant directed by the Chief Executive Officer only; the Board Officer is not a partner, holds no interest, and has no authority as a partner. **Section 3. Spending hold.** The company's standing spending hold applies in full: no expenditure except as the Chief Executive Officer directs, item by item, and all money moves under the Program Funding Policy as Article VI of the Bylaws and Article VI of the Operating Agreement provide. Article VI — Books, Records, and Confidentiality **Section 1. Records.** The company's books and records are kept as Article VII of the Operating Agreement and Article IV of the Bylaws provide: documentation first; every fact traced to its document; the Master Document Register kept current; every file in its own named binder in the Library; corrections by reversing or amending entry, on the record. The Sole Partner, as the only partner, has full access to all company books and records at all times; no partnership right of inspection exists in any other person by reason of this Agreement. **Section 2. Confidentiality.** The confidentiality law of the company applies to this Agreement and to everything under it, in full: client information is held at HIPAA-level confidentiality and is disclosed only on a client's signed Release of Information or a court order signed by a judge — a subpoena alone is not a court order — as Article V of the Bylaws and Article VII of the Operating Agreement provide. This Agreement itself is an internal company record; it is shared outside the company only as the Sole Member directs, as it is shared with the California Secretary of State with the letter that accompanies this package. Article VII — Admission of a New Partner **Section 1. The only door.** A new partner may be admitted to this company **only** by a written amendment to this Agreement, and to the Operating Agreement, signed by Levi Allen Spears, the Sole Partner, stating the person admitted, the interest admitted, and the effective date. Until such an amendment is signed, there is no new partner, whatever has been said, promised, or understood informally. **Section 2. Effect of admission.** If a partner is ever admitted under Section 1, this Agreement must at the same time be amended and restated in writing to govern a multi-partner company — shares, capital accounts, management, distributions, dissociation, and buy-out terms stated in full — before the admission takes effect. Article VIII — Transfers **Section 1. Restriction.** The Sole Partner may transfer all or part of his partnership interest only by a written instrument signed by him that identifies the transferee and the interest transferred. An attempted transfer that does not meet this requirement is ineffective to the fullest extent permitted by law. A transferee who is not admitted under Article VII receives economic rights only. **Section 2. No transfer by others.** No other person holds any interest capable of transfer, and no assignment, pledge, or encumbrance by any other person reaches the company or its Sole Partner. Article IX — Dissolution **Section 1. Events.** The partnership recorded by this Agreement dissolves when the company dissolves under Article XI of the Operating Agreement — on the Sole Partner's written decision, on a judicial decree, or on another event requiring dissolution under nonwaivable California law. **Section 2. Winding up.** On dissolution, the company's affairs are wound up under the Operating Agreement and Sections 17707.01 through 17707.09 of the California Corporations Code: debts and obligations paid or provided for in the order California law requires, client records preserved under the confidentiality articles, and remaining assets distributed to the Sole Partner or his successor in interest, as the company's only partner and owner. Article X — General Provisions **Section 1. Governing law.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of California, including the California Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act and, to the extent it applies to a claimed partnership, the California Uniform Partnership Act of 1994. Nothing in this Agreement creates a partnership among two or more persons where California law would not find one. **Section 2. Amendments.** This Agreement may be amended only by a written instrument signed by the Sole Partner, Levi Allen Spears. No oral amendment is effective. **Section 3. Entire understanding.** This Agreement, together with the Operating Agreement (re-created and restated November 11, 2026), the Bylaws (approved November 11, 2026), and the Declaration of Non-Partnership (November 2, 2026), states the company's entire partnership position. In case of conflict, the Sole Partner's written reading governs until an instrument is amended. **Section 4. Severability; counterparts.** If any provision is held unenforceable, the rest stand. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts and by electronic signature.
