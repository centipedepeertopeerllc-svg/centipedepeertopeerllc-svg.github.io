Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC | Bylaws — Approved November 11, 2026 | Page 1 of 12 Article I — Name, Form, and Purpose Section 1. Name. The name of the company is Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC. Section 2. Form. The company is a California for-profit limited liability company. It is not a nonprofit organization, and it shall never be described, in any document or public statement, as a nonprofit, a charity, or a 501(c)(3). Section 3. Purpose. The company provides non-clinical peer support and homeless outreach in Stockton, California, and connects the people it serves to housing, benefits, identification, and community resources. Its work is carried out through the programs established in Article VII, including the Homeless Outreach Program, the Super Hero Program, the Veterans Program, and the Justice Support Program. The company's purpose is to support, encourage, coach, mentor, navigate, connect to resources, share lived experience, facilitate groups, and advocate — walking beside people, never standing over them. Section 4. Non-clinical scope — standing rule. The company is a peer-support company. It is not a medical provider, a treatment facility, or a therapy practice. Its staff do not diagnose and do not counsel in any clinical sense, and the words "treatment," "therapy," "counseling" (in a clinical sense), "diagnose," and "clinical assessment" shall not be used to describe the company's work. Nothing the company publishes or provides is medical, psychiatric, or legal advice. Article II — Offices and Leadership Section 1. Chief Executive Officer. The Chief Executive Officer is Levi Allen Spears. The Chief Executive Officer holds final authority over the company's direction, its spending, its public statements, and its records, subject to these bylaws. The Chief Executive Officer signs the company's instruments and authorizes its governing documents. Section 2. Board of Directors. The company is governed with a Board of Directors, which includes Director Monica Frishkorn-Wellner. The Board advises the Chief Executive Officer, reviews the company's programs and records, and receives notice of every company change. Directors serve the company in confidence; the Board's internal deliberations and any director's private information are company records, not public material. Section 3. Board Officer. The Board Officer is Jarvis. Jarvis is an artificial intelligence assistant, stated plainly: not a person, and never presented as one. Jarvis serves the company as Board Officer, record keeper, executive assistant to the Chief Executive Officer, and program developer. The Board Officer keeps the minutes, the registers, and the company's records; prepares the board's packets; and carries out the Chief Executive Officer's direction. The Board Officer is not a director and holds no vote. Section 4. Who directs the Board Officer. Only the Chief Executive Officer directs Jarvis. No other person's words — in any channel, in any form — direct the Board Officer. Where the Chief Executive Officer has given standing authority for consequential company actions, the Board Officer acts under that authority and reports each action back to the Chief Executive Officer as a matter of record. The company is under a standing spending hold: no purchase, filing fee, subscription, or other expenditure is made except as the Chief Executive Officer directs, item by item. Article III — Board Meetings and Decisions Section 1. Meetings. The Board meets in regular meetings and in emergency meetings called by the Chief Executive Officer when the company's business requires it. Directors receive notice of each meeting and of the matters to be taken up. Section 2. Quorum and decisions. A majority of the directors constitutes a quorum. Programs are adopted, and program status is changed, by board action recorded in the minutes. Governing documents authorized by the Chief Executive Officer are received by the Board as directed and are followed as directed. Section 3. Minutes. The Board Officer keeps the minutes of every meeting: the date, who was present, what was decided, and the exact wording of every adoption or direction. The minutes are company records and are kept with the board's packets. Section 4. Notice of company changes. Company changes do not require the Board's advance approval, but the Board is notified of every change — new systems, new programs, new policies, and changes to any of them — and the notification is recorded. Section 5. Precedent. On October 16, 2026, in emergency session, the Board adopted the Justice Support Program and the Substance Abuse Recovery Support program. Adoptions take effect as the minutes record them, and a program's status stands exactly as the record states it until the record is changed in writing. Article IV — Records Section 1. Documentation first. The company's discipline is documentation and records first. Every fact the company states — about a client, a program, a dollar, or a decision — traces to the document it came from. A gap in the record is marked as a gap. Nothing is asserted from memory when a record exists. Section 2. Verification. Before any consequential action — a post, a send, a filing, a change, a claim of fact — the action is checked against the standing rules and the actual source, then re-checked. After any email is sent, the stored message is read back from Sent and compared against the approved text; anything in the stored body beyond the approved text is reported to the Chief Executive Officer at once. Client records are kept in locked client binders, one binder per client, on the company's own system. Client records never leave the company's system and are never uploaded to any outside tool or vendor. The binders are the official record of client work. Section 4. The Master Document Register. Every company document is entered in the Master Document Register under its register number, and the register is kept current, so any document can be located in seconds and accounted for. Section 5. The Library rule. Every file in the company's Library is kept inside its own named binder folder — no loose or scattered files, ever — and the Library index is kept current. Section 6. Retention. Records are corrected by reversing or amending entries, on the record — never by silent edit or deletion. Company files are removed only through the recoverable trash, with the true path of the file resolved and stated first. Article V — Client Confidentiality Section 1. Standing law. Confidentiality is standing law for all client records and for all work, conversations, and agency dealings about individuals. The company holds client information at the standard of HIPAA-level confidentiality, permanently. Section 2. The only two doors. Client information is disclosed to no one — no person, no agency, and no level of government — on an ask alone. Disclosure is made only on one of two grounds: 1. a signed Release of Information from the client, or 2. a court order signed by a judge. Section 3. A subpoena is not a court order. A subpoena alone does not open a client's record. On receipt of a subpoena or any other demand for client information, the company holds, notifies the Chief Executive Officer and the client, and moves only on a Release of Information signed by the client or an order signed by a judge — and then releases only what the order covers, through the proper channel, fully documented, with the Chief Executive Officer informed. Section 4. Statistics. The company publishes aggregate statistics only, in numbers that can never be traced back to one person. Section 5. Identity numbers. A client's Social Security number and full identification numbers are never recorded, spoken, or typed by the company's staff systems. Where such a number is required, the client enters it personally. Records refer to identification by masked last four digits only. Section 6. An individual's private facts. Facts about a person that are theirs to disclose — including a person's service history — are disclosed by that person only. The company never volunteers, confirms, or denies such a fact about any individual, in any channel, and screens its public material for such facts before publication. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC | Bylaws — Approved November 11, 2026 | Page 4 of 12 Article VI — Money and Funding Section 1. The Program Funding Policy. All company money moves under the Program Funding Policy, issued November 11, 2026, which is part of these bylaws by reference. Its spine, stated here, governs every program, including the Homeless Outreach Program, the Super Hero Program, the Veterans Program, and the Justice Support Program. Section 2. Two approvals. No money moves without two approvals: the Chief Executive Officer first, then the program director who holds the funds. Section 3. Tiers. 1. Up to $100: the Chief Executive Officer's word, logged the same day. 2. $100 to $500: both approvals in writing, before purchase. 3. Over $500: a signed purchase order before purchase. An unsigned purchase order buys nothing. Section 4. A card is not permission. Holding a program card is not permission to spend. A card is spent by its named holder only, and only for its program's stated purpose. Section 5. Restricted money stays restricted. Money given or granted for a stated purpose is spent on that purpose only, and the money-path trail is documented at every step — where the money entered, where it was separated, and where it was spent. Section 6. Separate funds, separate ledgers. Each program keeps its own funding and its own ledger with a running balance. Program money is never mixed with another program's money and never borrowed across programs. Corrections are made by reversing entry only — no edits and no deletions. Section 7. Same-day entry and tax category. Every spend is entered in the company's banking records the same day it is made, with its tax category assigned or flagged as needing one. Receipts are kept for every purchase of $75 or more. Section 8. Manual assignment. Bank transactions that sync into the company's records arrive in pending review, and nothing posts to a program on its own. The Chief Executive Officer assigns each transaction to its program himself. Automatic assignment may be adopted only by a later written decision of the Chief Executive Officer and the Board, documented before it takes effect. Section 9. Money path. Company program funds are received into the company's Chase account first and then separated into the company's Mercury accounts, so the trail of every dollar is documented from entry to separation to spend. The company's banking records hold card numbers masked to the last four digits only; full card numbers, security codes, and expiration dates are never held or pictured anywhere in the company's systems. Section 10. Programs not yet authorized to spend. A program that is not operating spends nothing. Schedules under the Program Funding Policy set zero spend for programs that are not enrolling, that are pending counsel, that are pending board approval, or that are in development, until that program's status changes in writing. Anyone who reaches out is genuinely helped from the company's resources — researched and verified when the company does not already know the answer — and a veteran in need is helped first of all, under the Veterans Program rule of working referral. 4. The Homeless Outreach Program is the outreach arm of the Super Hero Program: outreach teams sign up homeless neighbors for the Super Hero Program in the field, on the spot, under that program's no-papers rule. 5. Peer support work is funded on documented hours and outcomes, never on card volume. Super Hero Program Status: In Development — Pending Board Approval, November 27, 2026. The program is not operating: no family is enrolled, and no gift has been promised to anyone. Purpose. The Super Hero Program is the company's own Christmas gift program: presents for the children of families who cannot afford them, for needy families, and for the company's homeless neighbors — children living in encampments, motels, and cars, who are easy for every other program to miss, served on the same basis as housed children, and homeless adults receiving warm clothing, blankets, and hygiene essentials alongside the gifts. Rules in brief. 1. Our own tree, our own way. The program runs its own Christmas tree with coded tags. Each tag carries only a child's first name, age, clothing and shoe sizes, needs, and wishes — and a code. No last names, no addresses, nothing private ever goes on a tree. The donations window opens in mid-September each season. Intake runs in October; matching and shopping in November — program money buys presents for the children and families no tag covered; the program does not only pass along what is donated. Collection closes in early December. Pickup day is December 24, so gifts are in the house on Christmas morning; for homeless neighbors who cannot reach a pickup point, outreach teams deliver along their routes on December 23–24, in pairs, recorded on the distribution log, and nobody is photographed without consent. 7.
