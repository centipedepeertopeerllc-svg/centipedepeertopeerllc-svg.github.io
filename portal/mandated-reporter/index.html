What This Is

California law requires certain people, called mandated reporters, to report known or reasonably suspected abuse or neglect. Two laws do most of the work. The Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting Act (CANRA), Penal Code sections 11164–11174.3, covers children under 18. The Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act, Welfare and Institutions Code sections 15600–15675, covers elders (65 and older) and dependent adults (people 18 to 64 whose physical or mental limits keep them from carrying out normal activities or protecting their own rights).

A report is not an accusation and it is not a finding. It is a phone call that hands what you saw or heard to the agency whose job is to look into it. Your job is to report suspicion. Their job is to investigate.

Who Has to Report — and the Centipede Rule

The statutes name categories. Under CANRA (Penal Code section 11165.7) they include teachers, health practitioners, social workers, employees of licensed community care facilities, and an administrator or employee of a public or private organization whose duties require direct contact and supervision of children. For elders and dependent adults, the duty is written more broadly (Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(a)): any person who has assumed full or intermittent responsibility for the care or custody of an elder or dependent adult, paid or not, is a mandated reporter, including care custodians — the staff of agencies that provide care or services to elders and dependent adults (section 15610.17).

“Peer Support Specialist” is not named as its own category in the child-abuse statute, so coverage there turns on what a person's duties actually are; the elder-abuse statute's care-and-custody language reaches much further. Centipede does not leave this to debate: by company policy (CPP-POL-2026-048), every Centipede staff member reports as a mandated reporter, in every program. When you work with our clients, you report.

The Standard — Reasonable Suspicion

You report when you have knowledge of, observe, or reasonably suspect abuse or neglect. Reasonable suspicion means the facts you have would lead a reasonable person in your position to suspect it — it does not mean proof, and you do not need to be sure. You are not an investigator: do not interview a child or an elder beyond what the moment requires, do not confront anyone, and do not wait to gather more before you call. Poverty or homelessness, by itself, is not neglect; the question is always harm, risk of harm, or unmet basic care.

What Counts

Children: physical injury inflicted by other than accidental means; sexual abuse or exploitation; willful harming or endangering of a child's person or health; cruel or inhuman corporal punishment or injury; and neglect — a caretaker's failure to provide adequate food, clothing, shelter, medical care, or proper supervision.

Elders and dependent adults: physical abuse (including sexual abuse); neglect, including self-neglect; abandonment; abduction; isolation; financial abuse — taking, keeping, or using an elder's money or property by wrong or fraud, or by undue influence; and other treatment that causes physical harm, pain, or mental suffering, including a care custodian depriving a person of goods or services the person needs. Self-neglect is reported to Adult Protective Services so the person can be offered help; it is not a crime by the person.

How to Report — A Child

  1. Telephone immediately, or as soon as practicably possible — San Joaquin County Child Protective Services at (209) 468-1333 (24 hours a day), or a law enforcement agency.
  2. Within 36 hours of the phone report, send the written report on form BCIA 8572, Suspected Child Abuse Report, to the agency you called. Complete the form even if some information is unknown.
  3. Tell the Chief Executive Officer the same day that a report was made. This is notice, not approval — the official report always comes first.

How to Report — An Elder or Dependent Adult

  1. Telephone immediately, or as soon as practicably possible — San Joaquin County Adult Protective Services at (209) 468-3780 or toll-free (888) 800-4800 (24 hours), or a law enforcement agency.
  2. Within two working days of the phone report, send the written report on form SOC 341 to the agency you called — for San Joaquin County APS, fax (209) 932-2641 or mail to San Joaquin County Human Services Agency, P.O. Box 201056, Stockton, CA 95201. If the abuse happened in a long-term care facility, the report goes to the Long-Term Care Ombudsman or to law enforcement instead.
  3. Tell the Chief Executive Officer the same day.

Note the clock is different from child reports: 36 hours for a child, two working days for an elder or dependent adult. Do not mix them up.

Nobody Can Stop You — and You Are Protected

A mandated report is your personal legal duty. No supervisor or administrator may impede or inhibit your report — doing so is itself a crime (Penal Code section 11166(i); Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(h)). Centipede never requires internal approval before a report. Reports are confidential, and your identity as the reporter is protected by law (Penal Code section 11167.5; Welfare and Institutions Code section 15633). You are immune from civil and criminal liability for a report made in good faith under these laws (Penal Code section 11172; Welfare and Institutions Code section 15634). The reverse is also true: a mandated reporter who fails to report commits a misdemeanor — up to six months in county jail, a fine of up to $1,000, or both (Penal Code section 11166(c); Welfare and Institutions Code section 15630(h)) — and a willful failure, where the abuse ends in death or great bodily injury, carries up to one year and a fine of up to $5,000.

Training — Everybody Gets Certified

Every Centipede staff member completes the state's free California Mandated Reporter Training — the General Training course at mandatedreporterca.com, run by the California Department of Social Services, Office of Child Abuse Prevention. The course takes about four hours and ends with a certificate of completion: save and print your certificate the day you finish, because the site does not keep it for you. Your certificate goes to the Record Keeper and is filed in your HR record. Current staff complete the training by December 18, 2026; new staff complete it within 30 days of their start date. On completion, the company also issues its own Mandated Reporter certificate under the Chief Executive Officer as trainer, bearing the Mandated Reporter seal, and a copy is filed in your HR record. Staff and volunteers whose work puts them with children in a youth-serving program also complete the training the law expects of youth service organizations (Business and Professions Code section 18975), available on the same state site. Training is company time — but the reporting duty itself starts on your first day, trained or not.
