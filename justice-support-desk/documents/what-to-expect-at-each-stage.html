WHAT TO EXPECT AT EACH STAGE

A Justice Support Program guide for members

Your case moves through stages. This guide explains each stage in plain language: what happens, what you need to do, what Centipede does, and what to bring.

Remember: Centipede provides peer support. We are not attorneys, and nothing in this guide is legal advice. Your attorney or public defender is the person who advises you about your case. Most of our members' hearings are at the San Joaquin County Superior Court, Stockton Courthouse, 180 E. Weber Ave., Stockton, CA 95202. You can look up court calendar and case information with us through the court's public FullCourt portal (cms.sjcourts.org).

Always bring to court: a photo ID if you have one, any court paperwork you have received, and a list of your questions for your attorney. Arrive early — allow time for parking and security screening at the courthouse entrance. Phones must be silenced inside courtrooms.

Stage 1 — Awaiting Filing

What happens: You have had contact with law enforcement or have been told charges may be coming, but the District Attorney has not yet filed a case in court, or a court date has not yet been set. Sometimes no case is ever filed.

What you need to do: Keep your phone on and your contact information current. Watch your mail — a court notice may come by mail. Tell Centipede right away if you receive any paperwork or a court date.

What Centipede does: We check the court's public calendar and case information with you, help you understand any paperwork you receive (in plain language — not legal advice), and help you get ready: ID, transportation planning, and connection to other supports.

Bring to Centipede: any letter, citation, or paperwork you received.

Stage 2 — Arraignment

What happens: This is usually your first court appearance. The judge tells you the charges, advises you of your rights, and asks for a plea. The court may address custody and bail, and will set your next court date. If you cannot afford an attorney, you may ask the court to appoint a public defender.

What you need to do: Be on time, dress respectfully, and address the judge as "Your Honor." Do not discuss the facts of your case with anyone except your attorney. Write down your next court date before you leave.

What Centipede does: We remind you of the date, can accompany you to the courthouse, help you find your department (courtroom), and sit with you in the gallery as support. We do not speak for you in the courtroom. Afterward, we record your next date in the tracker and set your reminders.

Bring: photo ID, your court notice, and any paperwork from your release.

Stage 3 — Pretrial

What happens: One or more hearings while your attorney and the District Attorney exchange information and discuss the case. Dates are often continued (moved) to a new date. Your attorney may file motions. Many cases resolve during this stage without a trial.

What you need to do: Come to every date, even if you expect it to be continued — missing a date can result in a warrant. Stay in contact with your attorney and with Centipede. Tell Centipede every time a date changes.

What Centipede does: We track every date and every change, remind you before each appearance, can accompany you, and help you keep your paperwork in order and prepare your questions for your attorney.

Bring: photo ID, all court paperwork so far, and your questions list.

Stage 4 — Hearing

What happens: A hearing is a court session where the judge hears arguments or evidence on a specific question — for example, a motion, a preliminary hearing, or a review of conditions. Witnesses may testify at some hearings. The judge may rule the same day or set another date.

What you need to do: Arrive early. If you are told you may need to testify or bring documents, talk to your attorney beforehand. Follow your attorney's guidance about what to bring and what to say.

What Centipede does: We remind you, can accompany you, help you get to the right department on time, and help you write down what happened and what the next date is before you leave the courthouse.

Bring: photo ID, court paperwork, and anything your attorney asked you to bring.

Stage 5 — Sentencing

What happens: If a case ends in a conviction or a plea, the judge imposes the sentence: this can include fines, classes or programs, community obligations, probation, or custody time. The judge states the terms in court, and you will receive paperwork spelling them out.

What you need to do: Listen carefully and get your paperwork before you leave. Ask your attorney to explain anything you do not understand. Bring your paperwork to Centipede so we can help you calendar every deadline and obligation.

What Centipede does: We can accompany you, help you organize the sentence paperwork into a simple list of what is due and when, and connect you to resources that help you complete your obligations.

Bring: photo ID and all prior court paperwork.

Stage 6 — Probation-Compliance

What happens: You are on probation and must follow the court's conditions: reporting to your San Joaquin County probation officer, check-ins, classes or programs, fees, and any other terms the judge set. The court may hold review hearings to check progress.

What you need to do: Never miss a probation check-in or a review hearing. Keep proof of everything you complete (certificates, receipts, sign-in sheets). If something goes wrong — a missed appointment, a lost job, a housing problem — tell your probation officer and Centipede right away, not after the fact.

What Centipede does: We help you track check-ins, court review dates, and program deadlines; remind you ahead of each one; and connect you to housing, transportation, and other supports that help you stay on track.

Bring to check-ins: whatever your probation officer has asked for, plus proof of completed requirements.

Stage 7 — Resolved-Closed

What happens: Your case is finished — dismissed, completed, or otherwise closed by the court — and any probation term has ended or been closed. Your time in this program wraps up.

What you need to do: Keep your closing paperwork in a safe place. Ask your attorney if you have questions about your record or about next legal steps — Centipede cannot advise you on those.

What Centipede does: We complete a Case Closure Summary with you, return your documents, confirm your referrals are in place, and celebrate how far you have come. You can still reach Centipede's other programs for peer support after your Justice Support case closes.

Bring: your closing court paperwork, if you have it.
