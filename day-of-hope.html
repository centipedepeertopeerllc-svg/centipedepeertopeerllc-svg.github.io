A Centipede Peer to Peer Program

Centipede Day of Hope — Program Model

Status: Adopted by the Chief Executive Officer on November 14, 2026. Centipede Day of Hope is a standing program of Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC, and we are building it. The Board of Directors is notified at its November 27, 2026 meeting. No date is set and no venue is booked yet — the date and location will be announced on this page when they are set.

The program

One day a year. Everything free. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC brings Stockton’s service providers, vendors, and community partners together in one place for one day, and the people who need them walk in and get connected the same day. Centipede is the convener: we organize the day, and each provider offers its own services at its own booth, under its own name.

A peer-run company

Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a peer-run company — peer support and outreach, run by people who have lived it. Our peer supporters support, encourage, coach, and mentor; they navigate and connect people to resources; they share lived experience and advocate beside the people they serve. Peer supporters do not diagnose and do not provide medical or clinical services.

We follow SAMHSA. Centipede’s peer support and recovery support work follows the guidance of SAMHSA — the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration — the national guidance our peer model is built on.

A family run company — what that means here

Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a family run company. Ownership and control stay in the family, and final decisions rest with the family. Family runs first — the people leading this company know each other and answer to each other. We build for the long term, not for quick gain. The founder’s values are the culture: honesty, keeping your word, and treating people right, every day. The family name stands behind the work, so the record is kept clean and every document is done properly. Communication is direct and decisions are made plainly. People are not numbers — the people we serve are treated the way this family would want its own treated; nobody walks alone. We are stewards of our community, and our success is measured by what we give back to it. The next generation is grown into the company step by step, taught the systems and the standards first. And the company keeps financial discipline — careful spending and clean books — because the money carries the family name too.

“Family Run Company.”

The day itself — nine areas

Welcome & Navigation — greeters and peer navigators who walk people to what they need.
Food — groceries and a hot meal, free.
Clothing & Hygiene — clothing, hygiene kits, diapers, and haircuts.
Health Services — provided by health providers at their own booths.
Housing & ID Help — housing resources and help with identification documents.
Recovery & Peer Support — Centipede’s own peer supporters: support, encouragement, and connection to resources.
Veterans Services — providers who serve veterans, at their own booths.
Family Fun Zone — a place for children and families during the day.
Stage & Community Recognition — music, speakers, and an annual recognition of a community partner whose work stood out that year.

Who we bring together

Health and dental providers; food banks and grocery partners; clothing and hygiene suppliers; barbers and hair stylists; housing and shelter providers; benefits and enrollment helpers; identification and legal-aid helpers; recovery and peer-support organizations; veterans’ service providers; employers and colleges; city and county agencies; faith organizations; and family and children’s services. Each one serves under its own name, at its own booth.

How the day works

Two kinds of booths: information booths for service providers, and merchandise booths for businesses and vendors. Providers pay a modest exhibitor fee, and sponsors cover that fee for small nonprofits that could not otherwise afford to be there — nobody who serves this community is priced out of the day. Sponsors support the day at set levels and are named with thanks. The day before the event, a community collection drive gathers clothing, hygiene supplies, and food, so the day itself starts full. The event runs on a permitted site, with event insurance, coordinated with the Stockton Police Department and trained volunteer marshals in visible vests, so the day is safe and orderly for everyone.

How it gets built

Step one: the program stands adopted by the Chief Executive Officer (November 14, 2026); the Board of Directors is notified at its November 27, 2026 meeting.
Step two: providers, vendors, and sponsors are recruited and put in writing.
Step three: a Stockton location, city permits, and insurance are secured.
Step four: the first Centipede Day of Hope is announced — date, location, and provider list published as soon as each one is set, and not before.

After the event, everything is counted and reported: how many people came, what was given, and what each provider did — the record kept the way this company keeps every record.
