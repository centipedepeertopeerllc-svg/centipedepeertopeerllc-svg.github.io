A Centipede Peer to Peer Company Seal

The Mandated Reporter Seal

This seal belongs to Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC’s Mandated Reporter certification, established November 18, 2026. It is carried by every Centipede staff member certified under the program, and it appears on their company letterhead alongside their own seal. Every part of it stands for something specific. This page says what it means, plainly.

What each part means

The gold ring — the company standing behind the duty

The ring carries the company’s name: Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC. A seal is a promise made in the company’s name, and this one is built in the same gold-ring family as the company’s other seals. When this seal appears on a letter, the company itself stands behind the duty it names.

“Mandated Reporter” — the duty the seal names

The bottom of the ring names the duty: Mandated Reporter. Under California law, a mandated reporter who learns of suspected abuse or neglect must report it — child abuse and neglect under the Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting Act (Penal Code §§ 11164–11174.3), and elder and dependent adult abuse under the Welfare and Institutions Code (§§ 15600–15675). It is a legal duty, not a company preference.

The Centipede tree — who carries it

At the center top is the company’s own mark: one person helping another up toward the sun — Support, Empower, Recover, Together. No One Walks Alone. The reporting duty does not sit apart from Centipede’s work. It sits inside it, carried by the same people who do the company’s peer support and outreach every day.

The shield and the star — protection, and the duty to act

The navy shield is protection: the company’s promise to stand between harm and the people it serves. The gold star on the shield is the duty itself. When a certified staff member sees suspected abuse or neglect, they do not look away and they do not wait — they report it, the way the law requires.

“Protecting Children & Elders” — who the law protects

The line beneath the shield names the two groups California’s reporting laws exist to protect: children, and elders and dependent adults. It is stated plainly, on the seal itself, so there is no mistaking what the duty is for.

Who carries this seal

Centipede’s Mandated Reporter certification is established by company policy CPP-POL-2026-048, adopted November 18, 2026. Staff complete California’s official mandated reporter training and are certified under the company’s program; the company’s trainer is its Chief Executive Officer, Levi Allen Spears.

By company policy, reporting is mandatory for every certified staff member — and the seal’s use is governed by company policy CPP-POL-2026-049: every certified staff member carries this seal on anything they write on company letterhead, alongside their own seal, so that anyone who receives a letter from a certified Centipede staff member can see the duty standing behind it.

What this seal is — and is not

This certification is Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC’s own training certification, issued under the company’s own policies. It is not a license or certification issued by the State of California, and it does not make Centipede a government agency. The training behind it is California’s official mandated reporter training, and the reporting duty it names is the duty California law sets.

If you suspect a child, an elder, or a dependent adult is being abused or neglected, you can report it to your county’s child protective services or adult protective services agency, or to law enforcement. In an emergency, call 911.
