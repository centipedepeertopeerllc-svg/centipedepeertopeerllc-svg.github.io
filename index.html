Our core values and beliefs

At Centipede Peer To Peer, our foundation is built on unwavering principles that guide every interaction. First, we believe every person has worth, no matter their situation. No one is “too far gone” or “beyond help”; everyone deserves dignity, respect, and a chance to rebuild. Second, lived experience is a gift, not a shame. Our team comprises individuals who have walked similar paths, offering understanding that textbooks simply cannot.

Healing starts with connection, not judgment or labels. We prioritize real relationships over clinical distance, fostering an environment where people feel seen and heard. Lastly, we meet people where they are, literally and emotionally, providing support on the streets, in shelters, or wherever it is needed most. Everyone moves at their own pace, and we honor that journey without pressure.

Centipede Peer To Peer was created with one purpose: to change lives through connection, compassion, and consistent support. Our hope is to make a real, lasting impact in two places — in the lives of the people we help and in the community as a whole. We guide individuals toward stability and hope, whether they are struggling with addiction, mental-health challenges, homelessness, or trauma, by supporting them in taking the next step, no matter how small. We actively break cycles of isolation, fostering connection, community, and a sense of belonging. Ultimately, we support recovery and life rebuilding, helping people find resources, build coping skills, and reclaim their lives.

Why Centipede Has a Board

Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a California limited liability company. The law does not require an LLC to maintain a board of directors. We maintain one deliberately, as a matter of governance.

A company that works in the open owes the public more than good intentions. It owes structure. Our board provides independent oversight of the company’s programs, finances, and record; reviews performance against stated standards; and ensures that every major decision is examined, documented, and defensible — before it is carried out, not after it is questioned.

The board’s charge is accountability in four directions: to our clients, whose dignity and safety govern our work; to our partners, including county agencies and law enforcement, who rely on our word; to our funders, whose resources must be accounted for to the dollar; and to the Stockton community, whose trust is earned in public or not at all.

Board service at Centipede is not ceremonial. Directors review outreach results, examine the written record, challenge assumptions, and hold management — including the Chief Executive Officer — to the company’s own standards: non-clinical peer support, honestly labeled programs, clean books, and decisions that can survive daylight.

That is why an LLC has a board at Centipede. Not because the law demands it. Because the work does.

November 11, 2026 — How the Centipede Banking Program Works

Board notification. So the record is public and plain, the Board notes here how the Centipede Banking Program operates day to day. No account or card numbers are published; the system is described by its structure and its rules.

One card per program. The company holds one Mercury Bank card for each program, and each card is specified in writing for that program’s use only. The Banking Program keeps a separate ledger for every account and every card, with a running balance, so each program’s money can be accounted for on its own — what it received, what it spent, and what remains — without ever being mixed with another program’s funds.

Nothing posts automatically. Transactions synced from Mercury Bank arrive in the system in a pending-review queue, marked as synced. They do not enter any program’s ledger on their own. The Chief Executive Officer personally reviews each synced transaction and assigns it to the program card it belongs to; only then does it post into that card’s ledger. Whether assignment should ever become automatic is reserved as a future decision for the Chief Executive Officer and the Board together, and will be documented before any change if it is ever adopted.

Statements are company documents. Monthly statements generated by the system are issued as Centipede documents: they carry the company’s logo and seal, a registered document number recorded in the system’s statement registry, and the statement of what they are — the company’s internal program-level record, reconciled against Mercury Bank’s records, which remain authoritative at all times. The system is an accounting tool only: it holds no deposits, moves no money, and cannot initiate a payment.

About the Mercury name. Mercury Bank is named in this system in plain text because it is the institution where the company’s accounts are held. Mercury is a financial technology company, not a bank; banking services are provided through its partner banks. The Mercury trademark belongs to Mercury, and the Centipede Banking Program is the company’s own internal system, not endorsed or sponsored by Mercury.

November 11, 2026 — The Tax Center: Scope of the Work

What it does. Every transaction across the company’s program cards and business accounts now carries a tax category and a tax treatment, so the company can account at any time for what is taxable and what is not: taxable income, non-taxable income, and movements that are not income at all on one side; deductible, partially deductible, and non-deductible expenses on the other. Its limits, stated plainly. The Tax Center tracks and categorizes; it does not prepare or file taxes, and it is not tax advice. Super Hero Program

Status: In Development — Pending Board Approval November 27, 2026.

The Super Hero Program is a NEW Christmas gift program being designed for adoption by the Board of Directors on November 27, 2026. It is not operating, it is not enrolling, and it is not soliciting anything at this time. In its planned form, it will provide Christmas gifts for the children of families who cannot afford them, and gifts and essentials for homeless people, reached through Centipede’s Homeless Outreach program.

Our own way. Centipede will run its own tree sign-up and its own toy collection, in Centipede’s own name and in Centipede’s own way. The design learns from the practice of established Christmas gift programs, including Angel Tree and Toys for Tots, and from school Christmas programs — it does not copy their names, branding, or programs.

How it is planned to work:

  • Tree sign-up and family sign-ups — families sign up their children for the tree and the gift list; each child is recorded by code, never named in public material.
  • Collection tubs and donation boxes — local stores and businesses will be asked to host tubs for donated Christmas items and boxes for small community donations.
  • No bell ringing. Centipede does not ring bells. We are not the Salvation Army, and bell ringing is not part of this program in any season.
  • Donations window — the community donations window is planned to open in mid-September of each season, so the program has time to build what it needs.
  • Gifts bought as well as collected — donated funds are planned to be used to buy presents and needed items, not only to pass along what is collected.
  • Pickup day is December 24 — families pick up their children’s gifts the day before Christmas, so the gifts are in hand on Christmas morning.
  • Homeless recipients — homeless people are recipients alongside children and families. They are signed up in the field by the Homeless Outreach team during regular outreach — no fixed address, no ID, and no income papers are required; the outreach relationship is the verification. Children living outside receive presents on the same basis as housed children, and homeless adults receive warm clothing, blankets, and hygiene items from the same collection. Delivery is by December 24 pickup where a person can reach us, or by outreach-route delivery on December 23–24 for anyone who cannot.

Two stages. Stage 1, Christmas 2026: if the Board adopts the program on November 27, 2026, only a partner-and-in-kind pilot may run — hosting, adopting, volunteering, and referring through established campaigns, and a capped in-kind drive for families Centipede already serves. Stage 2, Christmas 2027: the program’s first full season, planned to operate under a 501(c)(3) affiliate or under a signed fiscal sponsorship agreement, with the donations window opening mid-September 2027 and gift pickup on December 24, 2027.

Privacy & HIPAA Compliance

Your information is yours. This page states, in plain terms, the standard Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC holds itself to for every record we keep and every conversation we have — and the rights you hold over your own information.

Our Commitment

Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC is a California for-profit limited liability company. We provide non-clinical peer support and outreach — we are not a hospital, a health plan, a treatment facility, or a therapy practice. We follow the privacy and security standards of the federal Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) for all client records, together with California’s Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA), California Civil Code § 56 et seq. Where California law is stricter than federal law, the stricter California standard is the one we follow.

This standard applies to information coming to us and to information going out — in both directions, without exception.

What This Covers

This protection is not limited to paperwork or stored records. It covers anything a member shares with us in the course of peer support and outreach: what is said in conversation, on phone calls, and in messages, and anything we discuss with an agency on a member’s behalf — what is said, to whom, and how.

We do not discuss our members with anyone outside serving that member’s own needs. Conversations about a member happen only for that member — their recovery, their connections, and what they need.

What a member tells us is shared with an agency or with anyone else only with the member’s authorization, or under a court order — never on a request alone.

How We Protect Your Information

• Client records are kept locked. Access is limited: a person’s information is seen only by someone who has both a genuine need to see it and clear authorization to do so.
• Information is never shared out of curiosity, familiarity, or pressure. There are no exceptions.
• We use and share only the information needed for the purpose at hand.
• We do not give client information to any government body — county, state, or federal — on a request alone. Information is released only with the member’s own signed Release of Information, or under a court order, and then only what the order covers, through the proper channel, fully documented, with our Chief Executive Officer informed.
