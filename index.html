Winter • City of Stockton

Stockton Warming Centers — Winter

When winter nights turn dangerously cold, the City of Stockton opens overnight warming centers for anyone who needs a warm, safe place to rest. The City activates the centers during severe cold weather and announces each opening on the City’s website and social media — check the City of Stockton’s Severe Weather Resources page for the current activation status before you go.

These are City of Stockton warming centers. Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC does not run these centers — we are sharing this information to help people find a warm, safe place to rest.

Arnold Rue Community Center

5758 Lorraine Ave, Stockton, CA 95210
(209) 937-7350

Stribley Community Center

1760 E. Sonora St, Stockton, CA 95205
(209) 937-7351

Hours when activated

Open overnight, 9:00 PM to 7:00 AM (per the City’s Severe Weather Resources page).

What to expect

A heated indoor resting space with restrooms, water, and snacks. Warming centers are a warm place to rest overnight — they are not full shelters and do not provide meals or medical care.

Open to anyone who is without adequate heat or protection from the cold. No sign-up is mentioned by the City; availability can change with weather conditions and usage.

San Joaquin County • Behavioral Health Services

Mental Health Services — San Joaquin County Behavioral Health

San Joaquin County Behavioral Health Services (SJCBHS) is the County’s mental health and substance use services agency. Behavioral Health Services is a San Joaquin County agency, not a Centipede program.

Main office: 1212 N. California Street, Stockton, CA 95202 • Phone (209) 468-8700 • Website www.sjcbhs.org

How to receive services

Call the 24/7 Access Line at 1-888-468-9370 (also listed as 1-209-468-9370). The Access Line is the front door — they will talk with you, screen for what you need, and connect you to services. You can also walk in at 1212 N. California Street in Stockton.

Crisis help, 24/7

Crisis Line: (209) 468-8686
Mobile Response Team: (209) 468-2222
Crisis Warm Line: (209) 468-3585 (a supportive voice, not an emergency line).

In an emergency, call 911. The national Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is 988.

How Centipede can help you get connected

Centipede Peer to Peer, LLC can help you get connected. Our peer supporters do not provide mental health treatment — that is the County’s work. What we do is walk beside you: if you want help reaching Behavioral Health, talk to a Centipede peer supporter and we will refer and connect you — help you make the call, help you understand the steps, and support you while you get through the door. You can also reach out to us at (209) 871-3939 or the office at (209) 625-9863 and ask for help getting connected to Behavioral Health.

Levi Allen Spears Sr., CEO

A certified peer specialist and mental-health advocate, Levi leads Centipede Peer-to-Peer with a vision of transforming how communities support one another. His work is rooted in lived experience, professional training, and lifelong commitment to helping people reach their fullest potential.

What “peer to peer” means

It means support comes person to person — listening first, respecting each person’s choices, and helping them take the next step they are ready for. No one has to face homelessness, recovery, or a hard season alone.

Leadership

Meet the founder: Levi Spears Sr.

Discover the journey that led to Centipede Peer To Peer. Learn about the personal experiences and profound vision of our founder, Levi Spears Sr., and how his lived experience shapes our commitment to compassionate peer support.

What inspired me to start Centipede Peer To Peer

Centipede Peer To Peer grew out of my own life experiences — the struggles I’ve faced, the people who lifted me up when I had nothing, and the times I had to rebuild myself from scratch. I know what it’s like to feel overwhelmed, stuck, to fight addiction, mental health challenges, or homelessness, and to believe the world has written you off. I also know the power of just one person showing up, listening, and believing in you — how that kind of support can change everything.

I started Centipede Peer To Peer to offer the kind of help I wish more people could find: real support from people who truly understand, not because they studied it, but because they’ve lived it. My journey taught me that people don’t need perfection — they need presence. They need someone who won’t judge, rush, or talk down to them. Someone who sees their potential even when they can’t. That belief is at the heart of everything we do here.

“Levi Spears Sr. exemplifies true leadership and compassion. His dedication to Centipede Peer To Peer is transforming lives and building a stronger community.”

— Community Advocate

Our core values and beliefs

At Centipede Peer To Peer, our foundation is built on unwavering principles that guide every interaction. First, we believe every person has worth, no matter their situation. No one is “too far gone” or “beyond help”; everyone deserves dignity, respect, and a chance to rebuild. Second, lived experience is a gift, not a shame. Our team comprises individuals who have walked similar paths, offering understanding that textbooks simply cannot.

Healing starts with connection, not judgment or labels. We prioritize real relationships over clinical distance, fostering an environment where people feel seen and heard. Lastly, we meet people where they are, literally and emotionally, providing support on the streets, in shelters, or wherever it is needed most. Everyone moves at their own pace, and we honor that journey without pressure.

How we hope to make a real difference

Centipede Peer To Peer was created with one purpose: to change lives through connection, compassion, and consistent support. Our hope is to make a real, lasting impact in two places — in the lives of the people we help and in the community as a whole. We help people feel seen, heard, and valued, knowing that many we meet feel invisible or forgotten.

We guide individuals toward stability and hope, whether they are struggling with addiction, mental-health challenges, homelessness, or trauma, by supporting them in taking the next step, no matter how small. We actively break cycles of isolation, fostering connection, community, and a sense of belonging. Ultimately, we support recovery and life rebuilding, helping people find resources, build coping skills, and reclaim their lives.
